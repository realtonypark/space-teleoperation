# H09: Ground-side phantom arm shifts the success-vs-RTT knee right by the machine-loop delay

**Axis:** latency-hiding control. **Author angle:** predictive display: ground-side forward simulation by RTT so the operator acts on predicted state.

## Claim

Letting the operator act on a ground-side prediction of the arm (its own in-flight setpoints) plus a dead-reckoned object, instead of on delayed telemetry, removes link and playout delay from the operator's loop. This raises capture success and demos/hour by >10 % at RTT ≥ 250 ms and on `geo_relay`, and by <5 % on `leo_relay` nominal, where the human model dominates.

## Mechanism

The testbed's failure mode is overshoot: the operator commands motion until it *sees* arrival, overshoots by speed × loop delay, and bumps the box away (`ground/operators.py`). Loop delay = uplink + playout + downlink + telemetry dwell + human reaction. A phantom arm drawn at the operator's newest setpoint is where the real arm will be when that setpoint lands, because the joints are position-servoed and the jaw command rides in the same packets. The operator eases off on the phantom; the real arm arrives one machine loop later without overshoot. The object ignores commands, so it is dead-reckoned from finite-differenced telemetry; at ~1 cm/s drift that is millimetres inside the 50 mm envelope.

Evidence (teleop_fundamentals §2): Kim & Bejczy 1993, several-second delay, phantom overlay gave ±5 mm placement in a ±12 mm margin (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/258061/). SARPD at 1 s: completion time −19 % (https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08627). Low-cost predictive display at 500 ms: speed +29 %, path deviation −35 % (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343874282). Teledriving at 150 ms, 29 participants: no benefit (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12788196/). ROTEX and ETS-VII used it at 5–7 s (space_teleop_prior_art §1). It hides the machine part of the loop and nothing else.

## Expected effect (quantified)

Human model 250 ms, playout 20 ms, dwell ~17 ms, operator speed 7 cm/s:

| Cell | Baseline loop / overshoot | Phantom loop / overshoot |
|---|---|---|
| `leo_relay` 48 ms | 335 ms / 23 mm | 250 ms / 17.5 mm |
| sweep 500 ms | 787 ms / 55 mm | 250 ms / 17.5 mm |
| `geo_relay` 600 ms | 887 ms / 62 mm | 250 ms / 17.5 mm |

Effective latency: −25 % on `leo_relay`, −72 % on `geo_relay`. On `leo_relay` nominal both overshoots sit inside the 45 mm close threshold, so success and demos/hour change by <5 % (predicted null, as at 150 ms in the literature). At 500 ms and on `geo_relay` the baseline overshoot exceeds the 50 mm envelope: baseline success 40–60 % `[estimate from the operator model, unverified]`, phantom ≥ 85 %, demos/hour +30 to +60 % (bracketed by +29 % at 500 ms and −19 % time at 1 s). Bandwidth unchanged. Safety events unchanged if the hold fallback works.

## How the testbed falsifies it

Task `capture`, `tau_h` 0.25, 50 Hz, seeds 0–29 per cell, `--max-s` 30. Cells: `zero`, `leo_relay`, `geo_relay`, plus the sweep at 100, 250, 500, 750, 1000 ms with `leo_relay` jitter, loss and outages. Strategies: `Baseline` vs `Predictive`. Ablations: object dead-reckoning off; hold fallback off.

REJECT if: (a) at 500 ms, phantom success is not ≥ baseline + 10 points and demos/hour not ≥ +10 %; (b) on `leo_relay` nominal phantom is worse than baseline by >5 points on either metric; (c) any unsafe-motion event appears under phantom but not baseline on the same seed; (d) the ablation shows the gain comes from object prediction, meaning the mechanism is wrong.

## Implementation sketch

`spaceteleop/strategies/predictive.py`, ~40 lines, `class Predictive(Baseline)`. `ground_step` logs `(t_send, setpoints)` in a deque and passes the target through. New `ground_observe(tel)`: if `tel.flags & F_SAFETY_HOLD`, or the echoed `last_cmd_seq` has not advanced for two telemetry periods, return `tel` unchanged. Otherwise return a copy with `q` set to the newest sent setpoint and `obj[:3]` advanced by finite-difference velocity over measured RTT + `interp_s` + dwell. `sat_step` is inherited.

It does not fit the hook as written: `ground_step(tel, target)` runs after `operator.step(seen)` has consumed the delayed telemetry, so the strategy cannot change what the operator sees. Fix is three lines: `def ground_observe(self, tel): return tel` in `base.py`, and `seen = strategy.ground_observe(seen)` before `operator.step` in `ground/loop.py`. The runner needs a `--strategy` flag, as every hypothesis does.

## Cost / complexity

4–6 hours: hook, strategy, one test (phantom equals last setpoint when the echo advances, raw telemetry when held), sweep runs. Runtime: one dict copy per tick. Satellite side: nothing; entirely ground-side, respects SYNTHESIS §5 choice (c), not the onboard-compute branch. Realtime cost ≈ 4 h for 8 cells × 2 strategies × 30 seeds.

## Risks and failure modes

- **Phantom divergence in outages.** In the 300 ms before hold is declared, lost commands let the phantom run ahead. Resume ramps at rated velocity, so safety holds, but the operator sees a snap 1.7 times/h.
- **Servo lag.** Phantom = setpoint ignores actuator tracking. Real Dynamixels need a first-order lag model with a tunable time constant.
- **Does not hide contact.** Insertion tasks 4 and 5 are exactly the phase the baseline is predicted to fail on, and this does not touch them.
- **Upper bound.** The synthetic operator trusts the phantom fully and consumes state, not pixels; a human needs a calibrated video overlay.
- **Low value at the design point.** Scored on `leo_relay` nominal alone, the effect is <10 %.

## Confidence

0.75 that it beats baseline by >10 % demos/hour at ≥500 ms RTT and on `geo_relay`: the overshoot mechanism is mechanical and the literature agrees at 0.5–1 s. 0.20 on `leo_relay` nominal: the human model is 75 % of the loop there. Worth running because the RTT sweep is part of every experiment.

NO HYPOTHESIS: Forward error correction or command repetition on the UDP uplink cannot plausibly move a primary metric by >10 % against the baseline on any profile in SYNTHESIS §7, because the command stream is already self-healing and loss is not what the baseline loses to. Each command carries the absolute joint setpoint, so a lost datagram has no decoder state to corrupt (unlike video, which is where RFC 8854 and the WebRTC NACK/FEC hybrid earn their keep, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8854.html, https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/41611.pdf); it is superseded 20 ms later by the next one, and the satellite playout interpolates linearly across the arrival gap by design (baseline.py), so the cost of a loss is a linear fill of a few mm on a 7 cm/s operator path. Simulating the §7 `leo_relay` uplink loss chain exactly as specified (GE p_gb 0.0034, p_bg 0.2, loss_b 0.3, plus 31 % of 15 s periods forced bad for 1 s; 2,000 episodes of 30 s at 50 Hz, script in the session scratchpad) gives long-run loss 1.16 %, longest consecutive-loss gap per episode p50 60 ms / p95 100 ms / max 160 ms, i.e. at most 11 mm of trajectory shape lost and refilled per episode; the 300 ms hold (15 packets) is never reached by loss alone (0.3^15 ≈ 1e-8), and `direct_gs` (0.15 %) and `geo_relay` (1.3 %, Analog-1 measured 1.27 %) are no worse. The events that do trigger hold are the Poisson outages (1.7 h⁻¹, 87 % of 0.3–2 s, network_emulation §1.1), which drop every packet on the path, so no redundancy inside one link recovers them; they touch ≈1.4 % of 30 s episodes anyway, a ceiling far below 10 % even if every one failed the demo. Redundancy also buys no effective latency: trailing-history redundancy (RFC 2198 style, one extra 28 B setpoint per datagram) only trims the jitter tail of a single late packet by ≈10 ms against a 333 ms loop (SYNTHESIS §8, ≈3 %), and forward "packetized predictive" repetition (Quevedo & Nešić 2011, https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2011.2159754) would either continue a stale trajectory into hold, which §6 defines as an unsafe-motion event, or add nothing before hold because the baseline already fills the gap. The dominant baseline loss is RTT-scaled overshoot (speed × loop ≈ 7 cm/s × 0.35 s ≈ 25 mm against a 50 mm capture envelope, operators.py docstring), and that is a latency-hiding or interface question, not a transport one. Bandwidth argues the same way: raw UDP duplicate-send is 58 kbps per operator and four operators sit at 233 kbps against the 256 kbps S-band cap on `direct_gs` (multi_operator_bandwidth §2–3), so blind repetition is the one transport choice that can hurt by adding serialisation delay on the pass regime. The only defensible finding from this lens is a controller detail, not a hypothesis: the current emulator reorders ≈13 % of consecutive uplink packets (exponential tail, jitter 15 ms, 20 ms spacing) and controller.py sorts its buffer by arrival time rather than sequence, so a late older setpoint is played after a newer one; keying playout on `seq` is a one-line fix that belongs in the baseline, and the §7 log-normal jitter with AR(1) ρ 0.25 will reorder at a similar rate.

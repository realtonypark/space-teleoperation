## H13

- **Sources check out, with one exception.** PMC12788196 is "Safety, Efficiency, and Mental Workload of Predictive Display in Simulated Teledriving": 50 vs 150 ms, PD "did not yield measurable improvements under moderate delay" (verified). arXiv 1809.08627 (SARPD, 10 participants, da Vinci) reports a significant completion-time decrease; the −19 % figure is from teleop_fundamentals §2, not the abstract (verified, number not independently confirmed). ResearchGate 343874282 returns 403; the report itself marks it "secondary", H13 does not.
- **The 150 ms null is a driving study, not manipulation.** Teledriving at 150 ms with 29 participants has no grasp event; the closest manipulation evidence in the report (MacKenzie & Ware, +64 % movement time at 225 ms lag) says free-space acquisition is already sensitive below the 333 ms loop H13 calls "inside the null band". The null band is asserted for the wrong task class.
- **The measurement is real but on the old sim.** I recomputed from the committed `docs/experiments/raw/{zero,leo_relay,geo_relay}`: episode ends 3.3/5.3/6.4 s (zero), 3.5/5.8/6.9 s at RTT 85–94 ms (leo), 4.1/7.6/9.4 s at RTT 510–513 ms (geo), hold 0 on all. My 10-seed re-run on the §7 `geo_relay` (612 ms RTT, old sim): 10/10 success, mean 8.13 s, 443 demos/h. The "capture v0 is not latency-limited" conclusion holds for the 50 mm envelope only; the uncommitted 20 mm envelope with a 2–5 cm/s box (chase error = speed × loop ≈ 7–15 mm at 333 ms) may flip it. H13 should be re-scored after that commit.
- **The tau_h artefact argument is correct and important, but it indicts the testbed, not the UI.** `ground/loop.py` hands the operator the newest frame older than `tau_h`; a phantom fed from commanded setpoints bypasses that frame and therefore removes 250 ms of modelled human delay that no real phantom removes. This is a required control for H09/H10 (compare phantom gain on `zero` vs `leo_relay`), and should be filed as a verifier rule, not buried in a no-hypothesis.
- **The resume-jump reading of `strategies/baseline.py` is right.** After a gap the interpolant's bracket is (stale packet, new packet) and `a = (t − t0)/(t1 − t0) ≈ 1 − interp_s/gap`, so the setpoint lands on the new packet within one 20 ms window. SYNTHESIS §5 item 4 says the ramp "is already the controller's behaviour"; it is not. `VMAX = 2.0` appeared in the uncommitted `sim/__init__.py` today, so the fix is in flight; H13's point that this is owed to the baseline, not to any hypothesis, is correct and also removes the outage-freeze half of H14.
- **Outage arithmetic is fine.** 1.7 h⁻¹ × 30 s = 1.4 % of episodes touched; the resume jump it would gate is a baseline defect. No objection.
- **Overlap.** Opposite verdict to H09 and H10 on the same mechanism; shares the resume-jump finding with H08 and H14. If H09/H10 are implemented, H13's reason (3) must be their control condition.

## H14

- **Richter et al. 2019 is cited correctly but points the other way on throughput.** Verified from the arXiv HTML: 750 ms RTT, 17 participants, "velocity scaling on average reduced the weighted error rate by 43 % and 29 % ... when compared against constant scaling of 0.3 and 0.2". The comparison is against *already-scaled* baselines, the metric is error, and every proposed method "required additional task completion time". H14 claims 1.3–8× demos/hour from evidence that measured a time penalty.
- **Hayes 1950 is paywalled; the bounds are standard and I reproduced the table.** 1 ms Euler of ẋ = −K x(t−L), K = 2: overshoot 10/19/32/58 % at L = 0.29/0.34/0.41/0.54 s (H14: 12/18/32/57 %); divergence at K·L = 1.60 and 1.78 (π/2 = 1.571). The linear model is right. The operator is not linear.
- **The baseline H14 predicts to diverge on `geo_relay` succeeds.** Committed raw data: 3/3 at 511 ms RTT; my re-run: 10/10 at 612 ms RTT, mean 8.13 s, 443 demos/h (old sim). Cause: `SyntheticOperator.servo` caps speed at 0.07 m/s and floors it at `slow`, so above 3.5 cm of seen error the loop is bang-bang, not gain-K; a 1-D simulation of the actual law gives 22 mm overshoot at L = 0.54 s and 40–46 mm at L = 0.8–0.9 s, inside the old 50 mm envelope, and the jaw closes on a 45 mm *seen* distance, so the capture lands before the chase. H14's expected +20–50 pp at RTT 250–400 ms is impossible against a baseline at 100 % success, and criterion (i) cannot pass. On the new 20 mm envelope the same arithmetic gives failure from ≈ 250 ms, so the claim may revive; it must be re-derived against the committed sim, not the linear model.
- **The strategy hook is the wrong seam for scaling.** `ground_step` sees only the operator's joint target; the operator's DLS step (`_dls`) linearises at its own integrated `qc`, which after scaling runs (1 − s) of the travel ahead of what is sent (≈ 15 cm of path at s = 0.34). The Jacobian is then evaluated at a pose the arm never visits. The honest implementation scales the operator's `speed`, which is a one-line change to `operators.py`, not a strategy.
- **L̂ is only measurable on the synthetic operator.** `now − tel["last_cmd_t_send"]` includes `tau_h` here solely because `ground/loop.py` hands over an old frame; a human's reaction time is in no packet timestamp, so the flight L̂ = RTT + dwell ≈ 0.07 s and `L0 = 0.29` gives s = 1 on every profile the program scores. The knob would have to be hand-calibrated per operator.
- **The outage freeze is not H14's.** The 14 cm lunge is real (`qc` integrates at 0.07 m/s toward a frozen seen error during an outage; the sat interpolator then jumps in one window, see H13), but the fix is SYNTHESIS §5 item 4 owed to the baseline and is already tested by H08. Criterion (iii) also needs a joint-velocity-at-resume metric that `metrics/__init__.py` does not compute.
- **Gain lives only where the program does not score.** H14 assigns 0.1 probability of >10 % on `leo_relay` and 0.6 on `geo_relay`; SYNTHESIS §2 ranks GEO relay 5th and §10 declares it out of scope for continuous control. A hypothesis whose author expects it to be "insurance, not improvement" on the acceptance profile is not a top-5 candidate.
- **Overlap.** H09/H10 attack the same overshoot with prediction instead of slowing; H11 removes the phase entirely; outage freeze duplicates H08/H13.

## H15

- **Sources verified.** arXiv 2304.13705: "8–14 seconds ... 50 demonstrations ... 10–20 minutes of data for each task, and 30–60 minutes in wall-clock time because of resets and teleoperator mistakes" (exact). LeRobot `il_robots`: `--dataset.episode_time_s=60` and `--dataset.reset_time_s=60` defaults (exact; note the same page's Python example uses `RESET_TIME_SEC = 10`). Astrobee NTRS 20180003326: "Each Astrobee accepts commands from only one Control Station at a time" and "During loss-of-signal with the ground, Astrobee continues to hold its position" (exact). Monsell 2003 is a review; the "hundreds of ms per switch" magnitude could not be fetched (paywalled), treat as `[unverified]`.
- **R is chosen, not measured, so the gain is tautological.** The macro runs "at the velocity clamp"; `VMAX` is a design choice (2.0 rad/s, uncommitted today). Gain = (E+R)/max(E, R+c) − 1 is >10 % for any R > 0.1·E, and the author picks R through the clamp. The REJECT clause "R_meas < 0.1·E" is the only way to lose, and it is under the author's control.
- **The testbed reset is a teleport, so R_meas measures the wrong thing.** The proposed macro is "home at the clamp, jaw open, `sim.reset` respawn". In orbit there is no respawn: the box floats at the target region after release, and the real reset is a carry back to the spawn zone (≈ 0.22 m at 0.07 m/s ≈ 3 s) plus a release the macro cannot verify. H20 makes exactly this point and builds the task around it. Either the macro carries the box (then it is a stored manipulation, see next bullet) or R in the testbed is a fiction.
- **The onboard macro strains the §5 decision.** SYNTHESIS §5 allows retract-to-stow and excludes "any autonomy that changes the trajectory". A stored sequence that transports a held object, releases it with a seeded nudge and settles is a manipulation primitive on the supervisor. H15 flags this as a risk; a verifier should call it: this is the onboard branch in all but name, or it is a retract that leaves the box at the target and does not reset anything.
- **Zero effect on the gated metric.** SYNTHESIS §6 gates on demos/hour *relative to zero-latency*. Pipelining resets multiplies both numerator and denominator by the same factor (H15 claims "+30–50 % on `zero`, `leo_relay`" alike), so the ratio is unchanged. It moves absolute throughput, which the program reports but does not gate.
- **The `direct_gs` edge terms are smaller than stated.** With the emulator's 540 s pass and E ≈ 8 s (measured), E_p95 ≈ 11 s, the straddle loss the admission rule prevents is ≤ E/2 ≈ 4 s per pass (0.7 %) and the gap-reset gain is ≤ R ≈ 5 s (0.9 %). H15's own formula (R + E/2)/P gives 1.7 %, not "+3–15 %". The 0.5 confidence on this leg should be ≈ 0.
- **Success-rate cost is untestable here.** The synthetic operator has no re-orientation; `switch_s` is a dead interval, which models the time cost but not the "−3 to 0 pts" success cost. Only humans can falsify that half.
- **Overlap.** H16 is the same mechanism (hide reset behind another arm) with the reset on the ground; H20 removes the reset instead of hiding it; H19 and H01 compete for the same `direct_gs` gap/pass time. Implement at most one of H15/H16/H20.

## H16

- **Sources verified, with two errors.** Same ALOHA, LeRobot and Astrobee quotes as H15 hold (see above). The ALOHA quote is attributed to data_pipeline §2.1; it is in §1.4 and §2.2. The "one write authority per arm granted by a ground broker" is a gloss: the Astrobee paper says a station takes control and others "may monitor ... and take control of them if necessary"; no broker is described.
- **Bandwidth arithmetic contradicts itself.** "8 arms on VR stereo fit the 50 Mbps `leo_relay` cap (52 vs 104 Mbps)": multi_operator_bandwidth §3 gives 4 operators × scenario C = 52 Mbps with FEC, which exceeds the 50 Mbps cap. The claim only holds for scenario A (10.4 Mbps) or B (39 Mbps).
- **The +25 % claim is not supported by its own table at the R the testbed will produce.** With the box respawned and only the arm streamed home (≈ 0.25 m at 0.07 m/s ≈ 3–4 s), the table gives +11 to +20 %. The REJECT threshold is set at +10 %, below the +25 % claim, so the experiment can "pass" while the claim fails. The same teleport objection as H15 applies: a ground-streamed reset that carries the box back is another ≈ 3 s of link time, which helps the arithmetic but is not what the sketch measures.
- **The runner sketch understates the rewrite.** `sat/controller.py::run_episode` is one episode per socket with `sim.reset` at entry and `Strategy.last` initialised per call; a pooled arm needs a session-mode controller that persists across episodes and hands the box off between a `ResetOperator` and a fresh operator without re-entering `run_episode`. That is a new sat loop, not "~80–100 lines in run.py".
- **Realtime fidelity with 8 arms in one interpreter.** The sat loop polls with `time.sleep(0.001)` and steps MuJoCo to wall clock; 8 sat threads + 8 link drain threads + 4 ground loops under the GIL will inflate dwell and measured RTT by tens of ms, i.e. corrupt the injected latency the profile is supposed to control. H16 acknowledges the GIL and defers to subprocesses; that launcher change must land before any number is trusted. Runtime 7.5 h realtime for one sweep.
- **No gain on the gated ratio; admitted.** "The acceptance ratio vs zero-latency gets slightly worse" is in the text. Also "successes per operator-hour" is not the spec metric (3600 / mean successful wall time) nor the data_pipeline §3 one (successful / Σ(episode + reset)); `metrics/__init__.py` implements neither reset charging nor per-operator accounting.
- **`direct_gs` gets nothing.** A ground-streamed reset burns pass time exactly like an episode; the gaps are unused. H15's off-link reset dominates H16 on the pass regime, and H16's "same gain on every profile" is only true because it ignores the gaps.
- **Cost is the scale branch.** 6–8 arms for 4 operators is hardware_landscape §3's scale branch and SYNTHESIS §4's "8 as the scale branch"; the hypothesis charges 8–12 engineering hours and not the flight hardware, mass, power or cameras that the extra arms cost on a 175 W bus.
- **Overlap.** H15 (same mechanism), H20 (removes reset), H19 (gap use). Independence 2.

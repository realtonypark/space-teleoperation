# H14: RTT-scheduled motion scaling on the ground command path

**Axis:** operator interface / workflow. **Author angle:** adaptive gain and rate limiting tied to measured RTT, to prevent move-and-wait oscillation.

## Claim

Scaling the operator's commanded joint increments by `s = min(1, L0 / L̂)`, where `L̂` is the loop delay the operator actually closes through (measured on the ground from the command echo, human reaction included) and `L0` is the `zero` profile's loop delay, removes the overshoot-and-chase failure of the capture task at RTT ≥ 250 ms and raises success and demos/hour there by more than 10 % versus baseline, while changing neither metric by more than ±5 % on `leo_relay`.

## Mechanism

The operator servos toward the *seen* object at `v = min(v_max, K·n_seen)` with `K = 2.0 s⁻¹` (`SyntheticOperator.servo`). Through loop delay `L` this is the delayed integrator `ẋ = −K x(t−L)`: oscillatory above `K·L = 1/e`, unstable above `π/2` (Hayes 1950, https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-25.3.226). With `L = τ_h 0.25 + RTT + dwell 0.017 + playout 0.02 s`, a 1 ms Euler simulation run while drafting gives:

| Condition | L (s) | K·L | Overshoot | Settle to 10 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `zero` | 0.29 | 0.60 | 12 % | 1.0 s |
| `leo_relay` (48 ms) | 0.34 | 0.67 | 18 % | 1.2 s |
| `leo_relay` 15 s spike (+74 ms) | 0.41 | 0.82 | 32 % | 1.4 s |
| RTT 250 ms | 0.54 | 1.08 | 57 % | 3.9 s |
| `geo_relay` (600 ms) | 0.89 | 1.77 | diverges | never |
| Any row with `s = 0.30/L` | – | 0.60 | 12 % | ≈3.4·L |

On `leo_relay` the baseline overshoot is 18 % of ≤45 mm, inside the 50 mm capture envelope, so the baseline should succeed there. From RTT ≈ 250 ms the arm sweeps through the box, knocks it and chases it: the regime where Sheridan & Ferrell's operators fell back to move-and-wait, one RTT per move (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1698160; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19660064449). Motion scaling replaces waiting with a continuous approach whose closed-loop gain is pinned at the `zero` profile's value. Human evidence: velocity scaling at 750 ms RTT cut weighted error 29–43 % (Richter et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03290); operators adapt to slowness but not to onset latency (Rakita et al. 2020, teleop_fundamentals §1.1), so the interface should impose the slowdown.

## Expected effect (quantified)

Transit slows by `s`; settling time is unchanged or shorter, so the cost is a fraction of a second per episode.

| Profile | Success vs baseline | Demos/hour vs baseline | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| `leo_relay` (s ≈ 0.89) | +0 to +5 pp | 0.95–1.02× | High that it is inside noise |
| RTT 250–400 ms (s 0.56–0.43) | +20 to +50 pp | 1.3–2× | Moderate; depends on baseline knock-away rate |
| `geo_relay` (s ≈ 0.34) | baseline <20 % of zero; H14 60–85 % of zero | 4–8× baseline, 0.4–0.6× zero | Moderate |

Effective latency and bandwidth unchanged. Safety: freezing the ground command when telemetry age exceeds the hold window removes the post-outage lunge, where the baseline blindly advances `v_max × outage` (14 cm for 2 s). At 1.7 outages/h this touches ≈1 % of `leo_relay` episodes.

## How the testbed falsifies it

Task `capture`, `--tau-h 0.25`, `--cmd-hz 50`, seeds 0–19 per cell, baseline vs H14. Profiles: `zero`, `leo_relay`, `geo_relay`, plus symmetric profiles at RTT 100, 250, 400, 500, 750, 1000 ms with `leo_relay` jitter. Log knock-away events (box speed > 3 cm/s while ungrasped) as the mechanism check. Reject if: (i) at RTT ≥ 400 ms, H14 success ≤ baseline + 10 pp or demos/hour < 1.1× baseline; (ii) on `leo_relay`, H14 demos/hour < 0.9× baseline, which rejects it as default-on; (iii) after a forced 1 s outage, H14's resume joint-velocity peak equals the baseline's. Confirmation also requires knock-away counts to fall where success rises.

## Implementation sketch

Fits the Strategy hook; the satellite side inherits `Baseline.sat_step`.

- `spaceteleop/strategies/adaptive_gain.py`, ≈30 lines: in `ground_step(tel, target)`, `L̂ = now_ns − tel["last_cmd_t_send"] − dwell`, EMA 0.2 s; `s = min(1, L0/L̂)`, `L0 = 0.29 s` kwarg; `sent = prev_sent + s·(target − prev_target)` on joints 0–4, jaw and slot 6 pass through; `s = 0` when `tel["seq"]` is unchanged for longer than `timeout_s`.
- `spaceteleop/run.py`: a `--strategy` flag, ≈3 lines; `episode()` already takes `strategy_cls`.
- Symmetric RTT sweep profiles: ≈6 lines in `link/profiles.py`.

## Cost / complexity

3–4 hours including the sweep script. No runtime cost. No satellite-side change; respects SYNTHESIS §5 (c). Not the onboard-compute branch.

## Risks and failure modes

- **Synthetic-operator artefact.** The synthetic operator never waits; humans move-and-wait above ~1 s, so their gain is time, not success. The testbed result is an upper bound; Richter's error reduction is the human-grounded floor.
- **Windup on a leader-follower rig.** The follower lags the leader by `(1−s)` of the travel; s < 0.5 may disorient a VR operator.
- **RTT estimate noise.** Jitter SD 14 ms modulates `s` by ≈4 %; a too-long EMA misses the 15 s spike, the one moment that matters on `leo_relay`.
- **Little to gain on the acceptance profile.** If only `leo_relay` is scored, this is insurance, not improvement.

## Confidence

0.6 that it beats baseline by >10 % on success and demos/hour at RTT ≥ 400 ms and on `geo_relay`: the mechanism is a linear-systems fact confirmed numerically, and the baseline gain sits past the instability bound there. 0.1 that it moves either metric by >10 % on `leo_relay`. 0.7 that the outage freeze removes the resume lunge.

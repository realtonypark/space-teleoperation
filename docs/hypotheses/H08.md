# H08: Send-timestamp scheduled playout with adaptive fixed delay

**Axis:** transport protocol **Author angle:** clock synchronisation and timestamp-scheduled execution (execute setpoint at t_target) to convert jitter into fixed delay

## Claim
Playing setpoints back in the ground send-time domain at `t_send + offset + D`, with `offset` a running minimum of arrival-minus-send and `D` the running p95 of excess delay (floor 10 ms, cap 70 ms), removes the time-warp and backward steps that arrival-time playout injects, and on `leo_relay` raises capture success and demos/hour by more than 10 % over the coded baseline with no unsafe-motion events.

## Mechanism
The coded baseline (`strategies/baseline.py`) sorts the buffer by arrival time and interpolates in that domain. Under the emulator's `leo_relay` uplink (mean 30 ms, exponential tail 14 ms) consecutive setpoints are spaced 20 ms plus a Laplace(14 ms) term, so executed velocity is the operator's times 20/(20+Δ): above 3× in roughly 10 % of intervals, and an older packet lands after a newer one with probability 0.5·e^(−20/14) ≈ 12 % [own computation; ≈15 % under the SYNTHESIS §7 log-normal SD 14]. Sorted by arrival, each such pair commands the arm back to the older setpoint for one interval. On a µg free-floating target every surge or reversal at the jaw is a bump with no friction to arrest it, the failure mode the synthetic operator models.

Timestamp-scheduled playout is the standard cure. RTP keys playout on sender timestamps with an adaptive delay from observed excess delay (Ramjee et al., INFOCOM 1994, https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/337676 `[URL unverified]`; RFC 3550 §6.4.1, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3550). Berestesky, Chopra and Spong (ICRA 2004, https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1308112 `[URL unverified]`) buffered time-varying internet delay into constant delay for bilateral teleoperation. No clock synchronisation is needed: the min-filter offset (Moon, Skelly, Towsley 1999, https://web.cs.umass.edu/publication/docs/1998/UM-CS-1998-043.pdf) is exact up to crystal drift (50 ppm → 0.25 ms over 5 s), and the testbed shares one clock (network_emulation §2 "Clocks"). The command header already carries `t_send`. Beech et al. 2024 (DOI 10.1145/3701984) bounds `D`: mean latency dominates jitter for the human, so depth must track measured jitter and stay under ~70 ms (teleop_fundamentals §1.3, §2).

| Sat-side playout latency mean / p95 `[emulator model]` | 50 / 78 → 58 / 60 ms | 35 / 41 → 19 / 21 ms |
| Backward steps per 100 commands | ≈12 → 0 | 0 → 0 |
| Executed-trajectory sum-squared jerk (`observation.state`) | −60 to −90 % | ≈0 |
| Capture success rate | +5 to +15 pp (wide: baseline level unmeasured) | ±2 pp |
| Demos/hour | +5 to +20 % | ±3 % |
| Velocity-jump events at 15 s spike / outage resume | >0 → 0 | 0 → 0 |

Bandwidth unchanged.

At SD 60 ms the baseline reversal rate exceeds 35 % and I expect ≥ +20 pp success.

## How the testbed falsifies it
Profiles: `leo_relay` and `direct_gs` per SYNTHESIS §7, plus a `leo_relay` sweep with `jitter_ms` ∈ {2, 15, 30, 60} at mean 30 ms, `reorder` True and False. Task `capture`, seeds 0–19 per cell, `--max-s 30`, `cmd_hz` 50, `tel_hz` 30, default operator. Log per episode: success, duration, executed jerk from `observation.state`, executed velocity reversals absent from `action`, sat-side playout latency, hold events, velocity above clamp at resume.

REJECT if any of: (a) on `leo_relay` with reorder on, success rate and demos/hour both stay within ±5 pp / ±5 % of baseline and the gap does not open by SD 60; (b) executed jerk falls by less than 50 %; (c) `direct_gs` demos/hour drop by more than 5 %; (d) any hold-resume jump or setpoint change during hold.

## Implementation sketch
- `sat/controller.py`: append `c["t_send"]` to the buffer tuple (one line; `Baseline` indexes `[0]` and `[2]`, unaffected).
- `strategies/scheduled.py` (~40 lines), `sat_step` only: 5 s deque of `t_arr − t_send`; `offset = min`, `D = clip(p95(excess), 0.010, 0.070)`, warm-up 30 ms; `t_play = now − offset − D`; sort `buf` by `t_send`, interpolate between bracketing send times, freeze at newest when nothing is ahead. Late packets insert in order and refine a segment: no drop logic, no catch-up burst. Expose `self.lag_s` for logging.
- `metrics/__init__.py`: jerk on `observation.state` as well as `action` (3 lines).
- `tests/test_scheduled.py`: jittered synthetic buffer; assert playout spacing equals send spacing and a reordered pair yields no reversal.
Fits the Strategy hook; the runner is untouched.

## Cost / complexity
3–4 h engineering, no runtime cost. Satellite side: a timestamp compare, a running min and a small percentile on the supervisor MCU. This is SYNTHESIS §5 item 1 ("timestamped setpoints, sort by sequence, fixed playout delay") made explicit, so it stays inside option (c); no Jetson involvement.

## Risks and failure modes
- Mean sat-side latency rises ≈8 ms on `leo_relay`; per Beech 2024 the human-loop effect may be neutral or slightly negative. The gain must come from execution fidelity.
- If the real relay is FIFO (SYNTHESIS §9 item 7) the 12 % reversal artefact vanishes; the `reorder=False` sweep measures what remains.
- The gain may land only on non-gated smoothness metrics, failing the >10 % primary bar.
- Percentile warm-up mis-sets `D` for the first second of an episode.

## Confidence
0.35 that it beats baseline by >10 % on success or demos/hour on `leo_relay`; 0.85 that it removes ≥50 % of executed jerk and all reversal steps. The mechanism is derivable from the emulator's own model, but no report measures manipulation success against command-side jitter, and the synthetic operator's sensitivity to a 2 mm bump is unmeasured.

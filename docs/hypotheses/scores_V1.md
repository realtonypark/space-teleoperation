## H01 objections

- **Geometry reproduced, so the cross-check is real.** Running `orbit.contact_windows` (sgp4init 550 km, i 97.6°, e 0.001, 5° mask, 10 s steps, 3 days) gives 4-polar duty 21.6 %, 32.7 windows/day, 9.5 min mean, 34.8 min median gap, 85 min max gap, matching network_emulation §1.4. A 45-site list of my own (KSAT/SSC/AWS/ATLAS/Viasat-class locations, coordinates from memory) gives 64.5 % duty, 66 windows/day, 14.0 min mean window, 5.6 min median gap, 30 min max gap. H01's 69 % / 63.9 / 15.6 / 4.5 / 36 is therefore plausible, not fabricated. Script: scratchpad `h01_check.py`.
- **The headline effect is arithmetic, not an experiment.** Step 3 ("fold") multiplies duty by demos per contact-hour; the only sim-dependent term is in-contact success at 80–95 ms RTT, which every cited curve (teleop_fundamentals §1.2, "ideal" < 200 ms) predicts to be flat. So the testbed cannot falsify the ×2.5 claim, only the geometry can, and that is a 20-line rerun that should happen before any episode is run. Testability 3.
- **Wall-hour vs operator-hour is the wrong denominator.** The program metric is demos/hour with 4 concurrent operators (PROGRAM.md scale row). H01 needs three regional teams, i.e. 12 operators, to reach 54–69 % duty; per operator-hour the gain is ~1×, as the Risks section admits. Compared at equal headcount (one team, EU only) the table gives 28.9 % duty and a 55 min median gap, i.e. ×1.4 not ×2.5. The claim should be restated per operator-hour or the 12-operator cost carried explicitly.
- **Does not move the acceptance metric.** Acceptance is defined on `leo_relay` (SYNTHESIS §6). H01 self-declares 55–75 % of `leo_relay` demos/hour. Its value is as a schedule hedge against open question §9.1, which is a mission-architecture argument, not a testbed hypothesis. Expected gain 2.
- **In-contact RTT of 80–95 ms is an estimate stacked on an estimate.** Kontur-2's 20–30 ms was ISS to Weilheim with the operator at DLR next door; no backhaul. H01 adds 49–63 ms mean backhaul from a 1.3 ms/100 km rule of thumb and unverified site list; provider backhaul (SYNTHESIS §9 Q6) is itself an open question. The 95 ms and 130 ms sweep rows cover this, but the "reject if success at 95 ms < 90 % of the 30 ms case" criterion is unlikely to bite for a synthetic operator whose latency sensitivity is a modelled `tau_h`, not a human curve.
- **Loss ×10 below 10° is underweighted.** The `direct_gs` profile applies ×10 loss below 10° elevation; with a 5° mask a substantial share of each long union window is below 10° (for a 30° max-elevation pass roughly the first and last third `[own estimate, unverified]`). The fold does not degrade those minutes; the Risks section mentions it in one clause without a number.
- **Hidden cost: uplink licensing and multi-provider scheduling.** An S-band transmit licence is per site and per country; 40–51 sites across 3–5 providers is a regulatory and contracting programme in itself, absent from the Cost section. AWS no longer publishes per-minute rates (pricing page checked 2026-09-12), so the $1–8 M/yr OPEX bracket is `[secondary]` at best, as H01 marks.
- **Overlaps.** Direct-GS session scheduling and pre-LOS stow are the same territory as H03's "direct passes as a low-jitter session mode" (which H03 declines to claim) and SYNTHESIS §10's "passes as the high-quality tail". `PassScheduled` (stow before LOS, no new episode near LOS) is worth 30 lines regardless of the dense-network claim and should be split out.

## H02 objections

- **Emulator flatters hedging by construction.** `link/emulator.py` draws an iid shifted-exponential delay per packet. Real Starlink delay is "stable within a period" with 15 s-period shifts (network_emulation §1.1, Mohan et al.) and the §7 spec adds AR(1) ρ = 0.25; the min of two draws from a per-period offset of U(−5, +5) ms yields ≈3 ms, not the 14–17 ms the iid arithmetic gives. The RTT-p95 REJECT threshold of −10 % will pass in the current emulator and say little about the link. Evidence 3, Testability 3.
- **Hold-time test has a zero denominator today.** `Strategy.timeout_s` is 0.5 s and the current `leo_relay` outage is 0.4 s every 30 s; a 0.4 s gap never trips a 0.5 s timeout, so baseline `hold_s` ≈ 0 and "cut hold_s by ≥ 50 %" is a ratio of noise. The test needs either the SYNTHESIS §5 300 ms timeout or the §7 Poisson-outage layer, and H02 says "now and again once the layers exist" without making the layer a precondition.
- **Arm 3 is not implementable as sketched.** "Independent jitter but shared outage phase and GE state" needs the emulator to expose a shared outage phase and shared GE RNG across two `Link` instances; today phase and loss come from one `random.Random` per direction, so two `Link`s with the same seed share jitter draws too (fully correlated), and with different seeds share nothing. That is an emulator change, not part of the "30 lines".
- **`cmd_loss` metric breaks with duplicates.** `metrics.episode_metrics` computes `cmd_loss = 1 − cmds_rx/cmds_sent`; the sat's `ncmd` counts every arriving copy, so dual-path runs report negative loss unless the dedup happens before counting. Small, but the sketch does not mention it.
- **Spike synchrony confirmed, which removes the largest tail term.** Casparsen et al. (checked) state spikes occur synchronously across terminals during handover. H02 concedes timing correlation but hopes for magnitude independence `[unverified]`; the mechanism's honest expected effect is therefore jitter-only, ≈ −14 ms on 48 ms base, and H02's own numbers say that is −4 to −6 % of the operator loop.
- **Primary-metric effect is self-declared ≤ 10 %.** Success +0 to +5 %, demos/hour +1 to +4 %. Under the program's rule (top 3–5 head to head against baseline on acceptance metrics) this is an appendix sweep. H04's exposure arithmetic (1.4 % of 30 s episodes touch an outage) is correct and caps the outage-masking benefit.
- **Physical unknowns are all on the critical path.** Two simultaneous mesh attachments for one third-party spacecraft, two distinct Starlink satellites in mini-laser range from a 550 km co-altitude orbit, terminal power (SYNTHESIS §4 budgets 40–70 W for an unpublished per-terminal figure, so continuous dual operation may double it), and whether both ISLs egress the same gateway and PoP. None is resolvable in the testbed.
- **Overlaps.** H04 is the direct negation. The gain is only cashable by shaving the 30 ms playout buffer, which is the jitter-buffer axis (SYNTHESIS §10, any playout-depth hypothesis), so H02 is not independent of that axis either. Independence 1.

## H03 objections (to the null)

- **The arithmetic checks.** MacKenzie & Ware +64 % at 225 ms → 0.28 %/ms (verified against the paper); 9 ms saved → ≈2.5 % movement-time gain; ×21.3 % duty → <1 % demos/hour; downlink half-spike +37 ms for 140 ms per 15 s = 0.93 % duty. All reproducible from SYNTHESIS §7–8.
- **The null is understated, not overstated.** `direct_gs` downlink of 15 ms assumes 10 ms backhaul `[unverified]`; H01's own backhaul table gives 49–63 ms mean RTT of backhaul on a dense network, i.e. ≈25–30 ms one-way, making direct-GS video *slower* than the relay's 18 ms for most passes. The hybrid would then cost latency, which strengthens H03.
- **The safety-hole argument is the strongest point and is correct against the current code.** `sat/controller.py` holds only on command silence; nothing on the ground stops commanding when telemetry or video stops. A split path exposes that gap in a way the single-path baseline does not. This deserves a one-line safety requirement in the spec (ground-side hold on telemetry silence) independent of H03.
- **One overreach.** "success rate, which the evidence places flat below 200 ms for both coarse and insertion classes": teleop_fundamentals §1.2 reports telesurgery impairment at 135 ms and even 50 ms, and the finger-force peg-in-hole study shows gains down to 48 ms. Flat for coarse tasks is supported; flat for insertion is not. It does not rescue the hybrid, because 9 ms is inside noise either way.
- **Bitrate-to-success gap is real but the testbed cannot see it anyway.** The video budget is bytes, not pixels (TESTBED_SPEC), so any picture-quality claim is unfalsifiable here; H03 is right to call that out rather than score it.
- **Overlaps.** Shares H01's direct-GS territory and H04's shape (null on link-path diversification). The undeclared idea at the end (direct passes as a low-jitter session mode for insertion tasks) is H01's `PassScheduled` plus task routing, and should be registered by whoever owns the scheduling axis.

## H04 objections (to the null)

- **The bounds are correct.** E[min of two iid Gaussians] = μ − σ/√π ≈ μ − 0.56σ → 7.9 + 6.2 ≈ 14 ms; outage exposure 1.7 h⁻¹ × (30 s + ~1 s) / 3600 ≈ 1.5 % of episodes, ≈5.7 % at 120 s; spike synchrony across terminals confirmed in Casparsen et al. The ≤ 10 % ceiling on success and demos/hour stands.
- **It argues past H02.** H02 explicitly claims RTT p95 −10 % and hold events −90 %, and explicitly disclaims a >10 % move on success or demos/hour. H04 rebuts a claim H02 did not make; the substantive disagreement is only whether secondary link metrics justify a strategy slot. Under the program rules (acceptance = success, demos/hour, safety) H04's conclusion is right, but it should say so instead of framing H02 as wrong on primary metrics.
- **"Same-path temporal duplicate-send already covers the bursts" is asserted, not shown.** In the GE bad state (`loss_b` 0.3) duplicate-send leaves 9 % per-setpoint loss for the 1 s window; the baseline interpolator freezes at the newest setpoint across gaps of a few packets, so the effect is probably invisible, but "adds nothing measurable" needs the one run it would take to show it. Cheap to close.
- **Shared base delay is an assumption.** Two terminals attach to two different Starlink satellites, so the mesh route (1–3 hops at 3–13 ms each, network_emulation §1.2 "stage effect") differs per path; first-arrival exploits that variance as well as jitter. It changes nothing above 10 % but H04's "(1) both paths share the 30/18 ms base" is stated as fact.
- **"Starlink + direct GS saves ≈18 ms RTT in-pass" inherits the 10 ms backhaul assumption.** With H01's backhaul numbers the saving is ≈0 or negative. Minor.
- **The constructive proposal is the right disposition.** An outage-rate sensitivity sweep (1.7, 5, 12 h⁻¹) inside the baseline experiment answers "how much would a second terminal be worth" without a new strategy and also serves open question §9.1. Recommend adopting it and attaching H02's dual-path arm as one row of that sweep.
- **Overlaps.** Direct negation of H02; same territory as H03. Independence 1.

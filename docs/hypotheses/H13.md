NO HYPOTHESIS: A latency-aware UI (phantom overlay of the commanded arm pose, RTT readout, link-state gating of the grasp and of the post-outage resume) cannot plausibly be worth >10 % on a primary metric on the target profile, for three reasons that reinforce each other. (1) The evidence for predictive display is all at long delay: −19 % completion time at 1 s (https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08627), +29 % speed at 500 ms (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343874282), and no measurable benefit at 150 ms (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12788196/); the `leo_relay` link is 48 ms RTT with a 163 ms machine loop (SYNTHESIS §2, §8), inside the null band, and the phantom does not hide contact, so it cannot touch the insertion-class phases (tasks 4, 5) that SYNTHESIS §6 predicts the baseline fails. (2) I measured the baseline in this repo to anchor the headroom (3 episodes each, seeds 0–2, tau_h 0.25 s, task `capture`, current placeholder profiles, raw output in the session scratchpad): `zero` 100 % success, 5.99 s mean, 600.7 demos/h at 10 ms RTT; `leo_relay` 100 %, 6.34 s, 568.0 demos/h at 90 ms RTT; `geo_relay` 100 %, 7.77 s, 463.4 demos/h at 511 ms RTT; safety_hold 0.00 s on all three. The whole link penalty on `leo_relay` is therefore −5 % throughput and 0 points of success, and the placeholder 90 ms is already heavier than the 48 ms SYNTHESIS value, so a hider that recovered every millisecond of link delay would still fall short of 10 %; the only place a phantom clears the bar is `geo_relay` and the ≥500 ms tail of the RTT sweep, which SYNTHESIS §10 declares out of scope for continuous control. (3) The one way a phantom "wins" in this testbed is spurious: `SyntheticOperator` servos on the arm position it sees and its felt lag is dominated by tau_h (250 ms) rather than the link, so replacing seen `q` with the commanded setpoint removes the human reaction delay from the arm channel and would beat the baseline by roughly the same margin on `zero` as on `leo_relay`, a gain against the operator model, not against the link, that any verifier would reject; Rakita 2020 (https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2020/RMG20/rmg-hri2020.pdf) shows onset latency hurts mimicry control even when users know their own hand pose, so the artefact would not transfer. The link-state gate half of the angle is also empty on this profile: with SYNTHESIS §7 values (1.7 outages/h, 87 % under 2 s) an outage falls inside ~1 % of 30 s episodes, and the resume jump it would prevent (verified by reading `strategies/baseline.py`: after a gap the interpolant lands at a ≈ 1 − interp_s/gap, a one-cycle jump) is already assigned to the onboard baseline as SYNTHESIS §5 item 4, so closing it is an implementation fix, not a hypothesis. Two program-level observations fall out of the measurement and belong to whoever owns the testbed: the 23 % throughput penalty at 511 ms RTT is far below the literature's ×2.04 time at 500 ms for coarse pick-and-place (teleop_fundamentals §1.2), so task `capture` v0 with the current synthetic operator is not latency-limited enough to discriminate any hider on `leo_relay`, and the profile values are still placeholders; until the task or operator is made latency-sensitive (insertion-class contact, a faster or noisier operator, or the SYNTHESIS §8 video pipeline modelled as telemetry delay) no operator-interface hypothesis can be falsified on the primary profile.

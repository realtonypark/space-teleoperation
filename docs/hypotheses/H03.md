NO HYPOTHESIS: A hybrid that keeps control on the Starlink relay and moves video to direct ground-station passes cannot plausibly move a primary metric by 10 %, and its handover opens a safety hole the baseline does not have. (1) The relay is not bandwidth-limited, so "high-rate video bursts" buy nothing measurable: four operators at two 720p30 streams need 10.4 Mbps down with FEC against a 25 Gbps ISL and a 50 Mbps emulated cap (SYNTHESIS §1, §7; multi_operator_bandwidth §3), no controlled study links bitrate alone to manipulation success (multi_operator_bandwidth §1.3; VISTA co-varies delay, bandwidth and loss, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08886), and the testbed models video as delay plus a byte budget, so a picture-quality benefit is unfalsifiable here (TESTBED_SPEC: "Video modelled as delay+bandwidth budget, not a codec"). (2) The latency gain is inside the noise: the direct_gs downlink is 15 ms against the relay's 18 ms, and with 2 ms jitter SD the 10 ms jitter buffer can shrink to about 4 ms, so the 333 ms loop drops to about 324 ms, plus removal of the downlink half-spike of +37 ms for 140 ms every 15 s, a 0.9 % duty (SYNTHESIS §7, §8; network_emulation §1.1; Casparsen et al., https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08439). At the MacKenzie & Ware slope of +64 % movement time at 225 ms lag, about 0.28 %/ms (teleop_fundamentals §1.1, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/169059.169431), that is roughly 2.5 % faster movement, available only during the 21.3 % of time a four-site polar network is in contact (network_emulation §1.4), so under 1 % on demos/hour, and no predicted change in success rate, which the evidence places flat below 200 ms for both coarse and insertion classes (teleop_fundamentals §1.2, §4). (3) The uplink stays on the relay, so every hold-inducing event is untouched: the +74 ms uplink spike each 15 s, the BAROC loss bursts in 31 % of periods, and the 1.7 outages/h (network_emulation §1.1). The reverse split, control on direct GS and video on the relay, would remove those only inside passes and is bounded by the same 21 % duty cycle at roughly 2 % of demos/hour, because the relay's stalls total about 100 ms per 15 s and about 2.5 s of hold per hour (SYNTHESIS §5, §7). (4) Splitting the paths decouples the two things the baseline safety logic assumes fall together. Today a relay outage silences commands and telemetry at once, so the satellite holds within 300 ms. With video on a fading pass (loss ×10 below 10° elevation, network_emulation §4 P1; pass ends every 9.2 min) the operator loses sight while commands keep flowing, and the handover detection window of at least 100 to 300 ms of missing frames is a period of blind commanding that no onboard timeout catches. Closing it needs new ground-side hold logic and per-path sequence handling (roughly 150 lines across ground/loop.py, link/emulator.py and run.py, since the emulator has one profile per direction and no contact-window or path-selection state) that exist only to pay for a link change yielding nothing. The one link-path structure with real headroom, using direct passes as a low-jitter session mode for insertion-class tasks rather than as a video channel, is a scheduling idea outside this angle and I do not claim it here.

## H17

- Rakita 2020 checked in the PDF: "no strategy emerged to overcome significant onset latency" (abstract) and 250 ms chosen "as the level of delay where performance starts degrading" (§4). The claim as cited is accurate.
- NTRS 19660064449 is Ferrell 1965 "Remote manipulation with transmission delay", not Sheridan & Ferrell 1963. Wrong attribution, right content; teleop_fundamentals §1.1 makes the same conflation.
- The sweep script exists (`cce55376…/scratchpad/sweep.py`) and does what is described, but it is in-process pure delay: no jitter, no loss, no hold, no 15 s spike. The settle gate is the one component that jitter and holds would perturb, so the segmented arm was tested under conditions kinder than `leo_relay`, which makes the negative result conservative in the right direction but not a socket-path measurement.
- The negative generalises from one protocol with slow constants (ballistic speed 0.05 m/s, confirm wait = self-measured delay + 0.5 s, minimum settle 0.1 s). A faster ballistic phase moves the 0.75–1 s crossover left; the hypothesis does not bound how far. "Cannot beat the baseline" is shown for this protocol, not for the design space.
- "leo_relay (89 ms RTT)" and "geo_relay (512 ms RTT)" were measured on the committed placeholder profiles (45/45 and 260/260 ms). The working tree now carries the SYNTHESIS §7 profile (30/18 ms; my run: p50 49 ms). The conclusion only gets stronger at lower RTT, but the numbers quoted are not the acceptance profile's.
- The verifier note is confirmed and already acted on: with the committed 50 mm envelope my 3-seed runs give 3/3 at 6.00 s (`zero`) and 3/3 at 6.19 s (`leo_relay`), a flat curve. The uncommitted `sim` now sets `GRASP_TOL = 0.020` and `close_at = 0.018`, i.e. H17's recommendation. Any hypothesis whose expected effect was quoted against the 50 mm task (H11 mechanism paragraph, H20 "45 mm envelope" in H18) needs re-deriving.
- Overlaps: H11 (the same terminal-grasp segment, delegated instead of gated), H14 (motion scaling is the other "slow the precision segment" lever), H13 (link-state gating of the grasp).

## H18

- UMI numbers verified in the HTML: latency matching on 105/120 = 87.5 %, off 69/120 = 57.5 % on dynamic tossing. Cited correctly, and the author correctly notes it is a different regime (hundreds of ms of gripper/robot misalignment on a release task).
- Skew arithmetic checks against `ground/loop.py`: rows pair `hist[-1]` (newest telemetry) with the setpoint just sent, so the recorded pair is off by downlink + ~half a telemetry period + uplink + playout. Note the code's playout is `interp_s = 0.02`, not the 30 ms of SYNTHESIS §5; the 85 ms figure uses 20 ms, so it is internally consistent with the code.
- The "under 10 % of the tolerance" conclusion was computed against 45 mm. With the working tree's 20 mm envelope the same 1–3 mm is 5–15 %, so the ">10 %" dismissal is now borderline rather than comfortable. It still does not make a testable hypothesis, because falsification needs a trainer.
- Phantom rows are larger than the text implies: my `leo_relay` run reports `cmd_loss = 0.051`, so about 5 % of recorded `action` rows were never applied by the satellite, plus hold frames. That is label noise a trainer sees, and the text folds it into "too small". Still under the bar, but it should be stated as a number.
- A second, larger skew is not mentioned: the operator acts on `seen` (≥ `tau_h` = 250 ms old), but the row records the newest telemetry. The record therefore never contains the observation the human actually responded to. Relabeling cannot fix this either, but it is the dominant obs–action inconsistency, not the 48 ms link term.
- The residual recommendation (log sat-side applied setpoint and `t_cmd_applied` per control cycle) duplicates data_pipeline §3.1 (`complementary_info.t_cmd_sat`, `t_obs_sat`, `link_state`) and is the same logging that H07/H08 need for their timestamps. It should be one recorder task owned by the runner, as the author says.
- No unsafe-motion, latency or throughput lever exists here; the "NO HYPOTHESIS" call is correct.

## H19

- MimicGen figures verified in the PDF: 82.3 % Stack (243 attempts), 52.1 % Coffee (192 attempts), §6.4; "In most cases, agent performance is similar" for 200 generated vs 200 human demos, §6.2; Gear Assembly D1 8.2 % generation rate, Frame Assembly 36.7 % agent rate (Table, §6). Orbital Express release verified: "several ground-based 'approval to proceed' (ATP) confirmations". The sources say what is claimed.
- Baseline arithmetic is wrong by 5×: "~30 s and ~90 % success" per human demo `[testbed-default estimate]` versus the measured 6.0–6.2 s at 100 % (3 seeds, `zero` and `leo_relay`, this session and the raw runs in `docs/experiments/raw`). A 6 s seed trajectory time-scaled 0.8–1.2 replays in 5–7 s, not 25 s. The multiplier changes either way, so the "4–6×" number is not derived from anything measured.
- Reject criterion (c) fails by construction with the code as written. `sat/controller.py` sets `held` after `timeout_s` without commands and passes an empty buffer to `sat_step`; a `Replay.sat_step([])` that moves the arm changes the commanded setpoint while `F_SAFETY_HOLD` is raised, which is the SYNTHESIS §6 definition of unsafe motion. The "controller untouched" claim is false: hold semantics must change, and SYNTHESIS §5 item 3 (retract after 10 s of silence) would fire 10 s into every 35 min gap.
- The reset is hand-waved in exactly the place H20 shows it matters. After a successful human demo the object is in the jaw at the target; each replay needs the object back at the seed pose ±2 cm. In the testbed `sim.reset` teleports it ("object pose from sim.reset stands in for onboard vision"), so the perturbation distribution is chosen by the author, not produced by a physical release. Replay success measured this way says nothing about flight.
- Not a program metric. PROGRAM.md's goal is human teleoperation data; teleop_fundamentals §1.2 classes replay output as "robot-executed, not human motion"; SYNTHESIS §6 reports `direct_gs` per pass, not per wall-hour. The author's own 0.3 acceptance probability makes the headline 2× a claim about a metric the program has not adopted.
- Onboard-capability decision: v0 (stored trajectory played autonomously for 35 min with no link) is "autonomy that changes the trajectory", explicitly excluded from the SYNTHESIS §5 baseline regardless of whether it runs on the supervisor. Both v0 and v1 are onboard-compute branch; only v1 is declared so.
- Hidden costs the text flags but does not close: arm duty 21 % → ~100 % against 4 arms at 30–80 W peak `[unverified]` and a 100–190 W continuous budget on a 175 W EOL bus (hardware_landscape §1); Dynamixel life at 5× duty; and per-condition runtime of 1.9 h realtime for a result the program cannot gate on.
- Overlaps: H11 (onboard branch), H15 (stored reset macro in pass gaps is a subset of this), H16 (ground-scripted resets), H20 (the reset problem itself).

## H20

- ALOHA verified in the HTML (§V-B): "10-20 minutes of data for each task, and 30-60 minutes in wall-clock time because of resets and teleoperator mistakes." LeRobot docs verified: `episode_time_s` and `reset_time_s` both default to 60 s. Both cited correctly. Neither is evidence about chaining or about microgravity; they only show resets are expensive on Earth.
- Tendon claim verified with MuJoCo 3.13: `springlength="0 0.20"` gives zero passive force at 0.10 and 0.19 m, and −5e-5 N at 0.21 m. An object leaving at 2 cm/s from 0.19 m peaked at 0.238 m and re-entered the slack zone at t = 12.9 s at 0.4 cm/s, matching the "≈13 s turnaround" figure.
- The tether does not bound the realistic failure tail. With k = 0.005 N/m and m = 0.05 kg, stretch beyond slack is v·√(m/k) = 0.063 m at 2 cm/s, 0.095 m at 3 cm/s, and 0.221 m at the operator's 7 cm/s approach speed (tendon length 0.42 m). The uncommitted cage is ±0.32 m in x. A bumped object at arm speed hits the wall before the thread acts; the thread only helps for exits at or below the object's own drift speed.
- The baseline is author-constructed. No document specifies a teleoperated return to a canonical spawn; SYNTHESIS §3 already defines task 2 as "capture and re-release". The measured gain is therefore chained-task vs a reset the author models (4–6 s carry back over 0.237 m at 0.07 m/s, checked), and the text admits "a baseline reset modelled too slowly inflates the gain." The ≥40 % is largely (reset time)/(episode time), a free parameter.
- Metric mismatch: testbed `metrics.aggregate` computes demos/hour as 3600 / mean successful-episode wall time and ignores resets and failures; data_pipeline §3.2 charges reset wall. The hypothesis needs the §3.2 definition to show any gain. That is the same metric change H15 and H16 require, so it should be one shared change, not three.
- The working tree already contains cage walls and a 2–5 cm/s box drift (uncommitted `sim/__init__.py`). "Today a bumped object drifts until `max_s` expires" and "no cage" are stale; the comparison must be tether-in-cage vs cage-only.
- Success-rate cost is under-argued: at the new 20 mm envelope a 3 cm/s target adds ≈1 cm of chase error per 333 ms loop, half the envelope rather than a fifth of it. The "−3 to −10 points" figure was derived for 5 cm.
- Overlaps: H15 and H16 (identical reset accounting and ALOHA/LeRobot citations), H19 (needs this reset to be meaningful). Independent of every Strategy-hook hypothesis, as the author says.

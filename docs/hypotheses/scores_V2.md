## H05: uplink FEC / repetition (NO HYPOTHESIS)

- **Evidence real.** RFC 8854 ✓ says verbatim "SHOULD prefer using RTX or Flexible FEC retransmissions instead of FEC when the connection RTT is within the application's latency budget"; the file quotes it correctly. Quevedo & Nešić DOI 10.1109/TAC.2011.2159754 returned 404 on Semantic Scholar `[unverified DOI]`; the packetized-predictive-control idea is real but the DOI may be wrong.
- **Arithmetic reproduces.** Re-simulating the SYNTHESIS §7 uplink chain (GE 0.0034/0.2/0.3 plus 31 % of 15 s periods forced bad for 1 s, 2,000 × 30 s at 50 Hz) gives long-run loss 1.10 % and max consecutive loss per episode p50 3 / p95 5 / max 8 packets, matching the file's 1.16 % and 60/100/160 ms. `0.3^15` for a 300 ms hold is correct; the coded hold is 0.5 s (`Strategy.timeout_s`), which makes the case stronger.
- **Baseline behaviour misdescribed.** The file says the playout "interpolates linearly across the arrival gap" on a loss. In `strategies/baseline.py` a lost packet gives a ≤20 ms freeze at the newest setpoint (t ≥ buf[-1][0]) followed by a half-interval step when the next packet lands. H06 describes this correctly. It does not change the conclusion (≤1.4 mm), but the two negative files contradict each other on the mechanism.
- **Bandwidth argument is profile-specific.** The 256 kbps cap is Kontur-2's S-band; multi_operator_bandwidth §3 says 8 operators at 100 Hz sent twice (1.7 Mbps) fit a 5 Mbps S-band uplink. "Blind repetition can hurt on the pass regime" holds only if the program keeps the 256 kbps `direct_gs` cap from SYNTHESIS §7, which it does, so the point stands but should say so.
- **Missed cheap falsification.** The negative is testable in under an hour: duplicate-send is a second `sock.sendto` in `ground/loop.py` and `cmd_loss` is already a metric. A NO HYPOTHESIS that could have been measured should have been measured (H17 did).
- **Overlap.** The one thing kept, "key playout on `seq`", is the entirety of H08's mechanism and is already SYNTHESIS §5 item 1 ("sort by sequence"). Conclusion also duplicates H04 (first-arrival over two links) and H06.

## H06: windowed setpoints per packet (NO HYPOTHESIS)

- **Evidence real.** RFC 2198 ✓ ("missing information may be reconstructed at the receiver from the redundant data that arrives in the following packet(s)"). Beech 2024 ✓ abstract gives "completion time impairments from 67 ms amplitude" and "comparatively small effect ... mediated by the mean latency". Valve Source wiki `[403]`; `cl_cmdbackup` is a well-known cvar but not verified here. RFC 6716 §2.1.7 not fetched.
- **Arithmetic checks.** "≥3-packet run in ≈2.7 % of bursts": 0.3·(0.3·0.8)² ≈ 1.7 % per initial loss × ≈1.5 losses per burst ≈ 2.6 %. Consistent. Freeze-then-step description matches `baseline.py`.
- **"Loss glitches never enter the dataset" is false.** `record` stores `observation.state` from telemetry, which is the executed joint trajectory, so freeze-and-step artefacts do enter the dataset on the observation side even though `action` is pre-link. Only the `metrics` jerk is computed on `action`. The sentence should be narrowed to "never enter the jerk metric".
- **Safety objection is wrong as stated.** Playing N extrapolated points is bounded to N × 20 ms (≈100 ms for N=5), which is exhausted before the 300 ms (spec) or 500 ms (code) hold threshold; SYNTHESIS §6 defines unsafe motion as the setpoint changing *while in hold*. Extrapolation before hold is not an unsafe-motion event by that definition. The "no gain" argument carries the negative; the "unsafe" argument does not.
- **Predictive-aid citation is stretched.** "Predictive aids return nothing below 150 ms RTT" comes from a phantom-overlay teledriving study (PMC12788196) about the human's display; it says nothing about sat-side extrapolation over a 60 ms uplink-plus-playout term. The 60 ms bound itself is enough; the citation is padding.
- **Overlap.** The kept note (K=1 piggyback replaces duplicate-send) is H05's subject. H05 and H06 should be one file.

## H07: priority state stream decoupled from video

- **Citations partly misattributed.** RFC 8837 ✓ marks *audio* EF (46) and data channels AF11/AF21; it does not mark "control" EF, so the claim "marks WebRTC control/audio EF ahead of video" is half right. The 1400–1600 packet drop-front queue is network_emulation source [3] (Karlstad + Malaga UDP bursts), not Mohan et al. Kontur-2 PDF fetched but unreadable (image scan) `[not verified]`.
- **BAROC figure is over-read.** network_emulation §1.1 (from BAROC, arXiv 2504.16322; abstract ✓ confirms the paper but not the numbers) gives −24 % bandwidth inside a *1 s window* in 30.7 % of 15 s periods, not "−24 % in 31 % of 15 s periods". A 1 s dip to 0.76× capacity against CBR video at 0.9× capacity builds ≈184 ms of backlog, which drains in the remaining 14 s. That is a transient of ≈0.2 s, not the "≈500 ms one-way, ×2.04 completion time" steady state the expected-effect table assumes. The 500 ms bufferbloat figure is from TCP downloads saturating the dish, a different regime.
- **Saturated condition is a constructed failure.** `frame_bytes` is zero padding; the synthetic operator reads only `q`/`obj` from the same datagram. Under `down.bw_bps` = 0.9 × video the baseline fails because state is queued behind dead bytes, and H07 passes because state bypasses them. A human would still be looking at video that is 500 ms late, and the file's own risk section says overlays give nothing below 150 ms. Experiment 2 therefore measures the emulator, not the operator. It also needs a `queue_bytes` field the emulator lacks (bandwidth is an unbounded serialisation model, `emulator.py` docstring).
- **Nominal gain fails the bar by the author's own numbers.** −16 ms on the 89 ms testbed loop is −16 ms on the 333 ms SYNTHESIS §8 loop (≈5 %), on the flat part of every curve in teleop_fundamentals §1. Confidence 0.3 that success or demos/hour move 10 %. Experiment 1 REJECTs only if felt latency fails to drop 10 ms, which is arithmetic, not a behavioural test. 12 ms of the 16 is dwell (1/(2·30 Hz) → 1/(2·100 Hz)), obtainable by raising `tel_hz` alone.
- **Priority may not survive the path.** Acknowledged in the risks: DSCP is not honoured inside Starlink's mesh, and SYNTHESIS §9 item 1 says ground-side allocation for a third-party satellite is unpublished. With the choke outside our egress the saturation benefit collapses to 4–26 ms of head-of-line blocking.
- **Off-nominal premise.** SYNTHESIS §1 and §7 size the downlink at 10.4 Mbps for 4 operators against a 50 Mbps `leo_relay` cap; saturation needs 8 operators plus rain plus a small allocation. The program's acceptance gate is on `leo_relay` nominal.
- **Cost is understated on the testbed side.** The sketch touches `proto`, `sat/controller.py`, `ground/loop.py`, `link/emulator.py`, `run.py` and `metrics`, not the Strategy hook; it is a protocol rewrite of the vertical slice, and every other hypothesis run afterwards inherits it.
- **What is worth keeping.** Splitting state from the video datagram and running state at 100 Hz is sound baseline engineering (Kontur-2 did it) and costs 121 kbps. Adopt it in the transport baseline, not as a ranked hypothesis. Overlaps H13 (overlay UI) and H02 (dual-terminal for outages).

## H08: send-timestamp scheduled playout with adaptive fixed delay

- **Citations.** Beech 2024 ✓ (67 ms onset, "comparatively small", mean latency dominates). RFC 3550 ✓ but §6.4.1 is the SR sender-report format, not adaptive playout; the relevant text is Appendix A.8 (jitter estimate) and RTP explicitly "does not provide any mechanism to ensure timely delivery". Ramjee 1994 and Berestesky 2004 `[429]` not verified; both are self-declared `[URL unverified]`. Moon–Skelly–Towsley PDF fetched but unreadable; network_emulation [25] cites it independently.
- **Arithmetic checks.** Reorder probability with iid Exp(14 ms) jitter at 20 ms spacing is 0.5·e^(−20/14) = 0.120 (Monte Carlo 0.121). "Above 3× in roughly 10 % of intervals" is 19 % including reversals, 7 % excluding them; order-right. But the coded `leo_relay` uplink is mean 45 / tail 15 ms (`profiles.py` placeholders), not the 30 / 14 ms the file assumes; the SYNTHESIS §7 log-normal with AR(1) ρ 0.25 is not in the emulator yet.
- **The mechanism is a baseline bug, not a hypothesis.** `controller.py` sorts the buffer by arrival time and `Baseline.sat_step` interpolates in that domain, so a straggler is played after a newer setpoint. SYNTHESIS §5 item 1 specifies the baseline as "sort by sequence ... fixed playout delay of 30 ms". Sorting by `seq` (H05 calls it a one-line fix) removes every reversal step and the "12 → 0" row of the effect table. What H08 adds beyond that is send-time-domain playout with adaptive D, whose remaining effect is a ≤20 ms time-warp per interval (≤1.4 mm at 7 cm/s) that H06 already argues is negligible and that Beech places under the 67 ms onset. The head-to-head is therefore against a defect, and any measured gain is not attributable to the hypothesis.
- **The proposed jerk metric is aliased.** `observation.state` in `record` is `hist[-1][1]["q"]` sampled once per 50 Hz command tick from 30 Hz telemetry, so ≈40 % of rows repeat the previous row. A third difference over that sequence measures the 30/50 Hz sampling pattern, not playout smoothness. Rejection criterion (b), "executed jerk falls by less than 50 %", cannot be evaluated as specified; it needs a sat-side log of the commanded setpoint at the sim rate, which the sketch does not add.
- **Adaptive D re-introduces the artefact it removes.** D = p95 of excess over a 5 s window with a 10–70 ms clip: every 15 s uplink spike (+74 ms for 140 ms, SYNTHESIS §7) pushes D to the 70 ms cap for the next 5 s, then it drops back. Each change of D shifts `t_play` by up to 60 ms in one control cycle, a velocity discontinuity larger than the 20 ms reorder step. RTP adapts playout only at talkspurt boundaries for this reason; the sketch has no slew or hold-on-D logic. Rejection criterion (d) (no velocity jump) is at risk from the design itself.
- **Latency table has the sign wrong in one column.** `direct_gs` 35/41 → 19/21 ms uses the SYNTHESIS 15 ms base with Gaussian SD 2, not the coded profile (25 ms, tail 8); against the coded profile the scheduled playout lands at ≈27 ms, not 19. Either way the file predicts ±2 pp there, so nothing gated depends on it.
- **Expected gain, honestly stated, is below the bar.** Author confidence 0.35 for >10 % on a primary metric, 0.85 for a non-gated smoothness metric. The success-rate row is "+5 to +15 pp (wide: baseline level unmeasured)" — the baseline `leo_relay` success rate has not been run, so the ±5 pp rejection band is unanchored.
- **Overlap.** Identical mechanism to H05's kept note; H04 and H02 also trim the jitter tail. Fits inside SYNTHESIS §5 (c), correctly declared. Recommendation: apply `seq`-sort and a fixed 30 ms send-time playout to the baseline before any hypothesis is run, then drop H08.

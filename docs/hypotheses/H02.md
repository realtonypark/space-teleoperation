# H02: Dual-terminal hedged send over the Starlink laser mesh

**Axis:** link path. **Author angle:** commercial LEO relay via optical inter-satellite links (Starlink mini-laser) as primary.

## Claim

Running the two mini-laser terminals from SYNTHESIS §4 as two *simultaneously active* paths, sending every command and state-telemetry packet over both and keeping the earliest copy per sequence number, cuts link RTT p95 on `leo_relay` by more than 10 % and removes more than 90 % of outage-induced hold events at no cost to success rate. It will not move success rate or demos/hour by more than 10 % on the coarse capture task.

## Mechanism

SYNTHESIS §2 treats the second terminal as a standby that re-acquires while the first carries traffic. Failover cannot help with the impairments the evidence describes, because they are shorter than any detection time: a +74 ms uplink spike lasting 140 ms every 15 s (Casparsen et al., https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08439), 1 s loss bursts with loss ×16 in 31 % of periods (BAROC, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16322), and outages of which 87 % last under 2 s (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13402). Hedged sending needs no detection: the receiver takes whichever copy lands first, so delivered delay is the minimum of two draws. This is the hedged-request pattern of Dean and Barroso (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2408776.2408794) and of Vulimiri et al., who measured tail-latency cuts from duplicating traffic over independent paths (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2535372.2535392).

Redundancy is nearly free: uplink is 27–54 kbps per operator (SYNTHESIS §10), state telemetry a few kbps; video is not duplicated. Starcloud flies two terminals per spacecraft (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260526670395/en/Starcloud-to-Integrate-SpaceXs-Starlink-Mini-Lasers-Into-Its-Orbital-Data-Center-Constellation).

Arithmetic in the current emulator: the minimum of two iid exponential tails has half the mean, so `leo_relay` RTT mean goes 90 → 72.5 ms (−19 %) and per-direction p95 from base + 3·jitter to base + 1.5·jitter (≈ −30 % on RTT p95). Under the §7 log-normal values (SD 14/11 ms) the mean gain is ≈ 0.56 σ per direction, ≈ −14 ms on 48 ms.

## Expected effect (quantified)

On `leo_relay`, dual-path versus single-path, both with Baseline playout:

- Link RTT p50: −15 to −19 % (±5 % measurement noise). RTT p95: −25 to −35 %.
- Hold events and hold time: −90 to −99 % if outage processes are independent between paths; 0 % if fully correlated.
- Operator loop (SYNTHESIS §8, 333 ms): −4 to −6 %. Machine loop (163 ms): −9 to −12 %.
- Success rate on capture: +0 to +5 %. Demos/hour: +1 to +4 % (1.7 outages/h touch ≈ 1.5 % of 30 s episodes).
- Unsafe motion: unchanged at zero. Bandwidth: uplink ×2, still < 0.1 Mbps for four operators.

`direct_gs` and `geo_relay` have no second path; no effect.

## How the testbed falsifies it

Profile `leo_relay`, now and again once the §7 spike, burst and Poisson-outage layers exist. Task capture, `SyntheticOperator`, `tau_h` 0.25 s, `cmd_hz` 50, `tel_hz` 30, `max_s` 30. Seeds 0–19, 20 episodes per arm:

1. Single path (baseline).
2. Dual path, two `Link` instances with independent seeds.
3. Dual path, independent jitter but shared outage phase and GE state (bounds the correlated real-world case).

REJECT if arm 2 does not cut `rtt_p95` by ≥ 10 % versus arm 1, or does not cut `hold_s` by ≥ 50 %, or lowers success rate or demos/hour beyond one standard error. DOWNGRADE if arm 3 shows no gain beyond the jitter-only prediction: the real-world value then rests on an `[unverified]` independence claim. If success rate rises by > 10 %, the mechanism above is wrong and must be re-examined.

## Implementation sketch

About 30 lines, no new dependency:

- `run.py::episode`: `n_paths` argument; build N `Link` objects with seeds `seed + 1000·k`; pass the up-port list to the ground loop and the down-port list to the sat.
- `ground/loop.py`: send each command to every up address; drop telemetry whose `seq` was already seen.
- `sat/controller.py`: send each telemetry frame to every down address.
- `strategies/dualpath.py`: `class DualPath(Baseline)` whose `sat_step` keeps the earliest arrival per `seq` before interpolating (≈ 6 lines); otherwise duplicates distort each interpolation segment.
- One test in `tests/test_link.py`: two links, duplicate send, p50 below single-path p50 by the predicted margin.

Strategy hook fit: the satellite half fits exactly. The ground half does not, because the hook has no transport seam; fan-out to N addresses is a runner and loop change.

## Cost / complexity

Engineering 3–4 hours including arm 3. Runtime ≈ 30 min wall clock for 60 episodes. Satellite side: dedup by sequence is stateless apart from a last-seen window, inside SYNTHESIS §5 (c); no onboard-compute branch. Hardware: both terminals continuously active, so terminal power doubles (SYNTHESIS §4 budgets 40–70 W, per-terminal figure unpublished).

## Risks and failure modes

- Reconfiguration is globally synchronised at 15 s (Mohan et al., https://www.nitindermohan.com/documents/2024/pubs/starlinkWWW2024.pdf): spike timing is correlated between terminals; independence of per-path spike magnitude and burst outcome is `[unverified]`.
- Both ISLs may egress through the same gateway and PoP, leaving the terrestrial segment undiversified.
- Two concurrent mesh attachments for one third-party spacecraft: terms unpublished (SYNTHESIS §9 item 1).
- Simultaneous line of sight to two distinct Starlink satellites from a co-altitude 550 km orbit: geometry `[unverified]`.
- Independent emulator seeds are an upper bound on real diversity; arm 3 shows the floor.

## Confidence

0.75 that it beats baseline by > 10 % on link RTT p95 and hold time in the emulator: the minimum-of-two arithmetic is not in doubt, only measurement noise. 0.4 that the gain survives on the real link, because path independence is unverified. 0.1 that capture success rate improves by > 10 %: the link is 14 % of the loop and the human 51 %. Worth running because the second terminal is already paid for and the change is 30 lines; it is not the hider the program needs for insertion-class tasks.

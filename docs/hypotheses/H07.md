# H07: Priority state stream decoupled from video

**Axis:** transport protocol. **Author angle:** telemetry prioritisation, state first, video second, delta/priority encoding under bandwidth caps.

## Claim

Sending joint/object state as its own small datagram flow at 100 Hz, strictly prioritised ahead of video at every queue we own, cuts the operator-felt machine loop on `leo_relay` by ≥15 ms (≈18 % of the testbed's 89 ms) and, when the downlink is saturated by video, keeps success rate ≥90 % of the unsaturated value while the baseline (state and video in one datagram, one FIFO) falls below 50 %.

## Mechanism

Today state rides inside the video datagram (`pack_tel` appends `frame` to a 123 B header), so state rate equals video rate and state waits behind every video byte. (1) Sampling dwell: at 30 Hz the ground sees a command's effect ≈17 ms late on average; at 100 Hz, 5 ms. (2) Head-of-line blocking: an 8 KB frame serialises in 4 ms at the placeholder 16 Mbps cap, 26 ms when eight encoders burst on one 30 Hz clock into a 20 Mbps allocation, and without bound once offered load exceeds capacity. Starlink queues are 1400–1600 packets, drop-front, and inflate RTT 2–4× to 400–500 ms under load (Mohan et al., https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09242; https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27717). Measured capacity dips that push a CBR video budget there: −24 % in 31 % of 15 s periods (BAROC, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16322), −38 % in rain (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04772). A state packet in that queue moves the operator from the 50 ms point of the latency curve to the 500 ms point, where coarse pick-and-place time is ×2.04 (teleop_fundamentals §1.2).

Strict priority for a low-rate control flow is standard: RFC 8837 marks WebRTC control/audio EF ahead of video (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8837), RFC 4594 defines the EF class (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4594), and Kontur-2 kept a 500 Hz control loop inside 256 kbps with tiny separate control packets (https://elib.dlr.de/105317/1/07487246.pdf). Delta encoding is rejected: full state at 100 Hz costs 121 kbps per operator (≈5 % of one 720p30 stream); deltas add loss dependence for <60 kbps saved.

The >10 % claim rests on effective latency nominally and on success under saturation.

## How the testbed falsifies it

Task `capture`, `tau_h` 0.25 s, `cmd_hz` 50, seeds 0–19, 20 episodes per arm: baseline (`tel_hz` 30, `frame_bytes` 8192) vs H07 (state 100 Hz, video 30 Hz at 8192 B). Log felt latency as `rtt_ms` plus sat dwell per row.

1. `zero` and `leo_relay` nominal. REJECT if felt latency does not fall ≥10 ms on `leo_relay`, or success or demos/hour fall on either.
2. `leo_relay` with `down.bw_bps` ≈ 224 kB/s (0.9 × 8315 × 30) and a 112 kB drop-front queue. REJECT if H07 success is not ≥1.3× baseline, or baseline does not degrade (emulator queue not biting; premise void here).
3. `direct_gs` reported, not gated.

## Implementation sketch

Cannot fit the Strategy hook alone: `Strategy` governs setpoints, not packetisation. ≈70 lines:

- `proto`: state datagram = existing 123 B header with `frame_len` 0; new `STV1` video datagram (seq, t_send, bytes). ~12 lines.
- `sat/controller.py`: emit state at `state_hz` (100) and video at `tel_hz`; the 1 ms loop already supports it. ~10 lines.
- `ground/loop.py`: accept both; `hist`, RTT and the operator use state; video counted only. ~8 lines.
- `link/emulator.py`: `bw_bps` serialisation and a new `queue_bytes` drop-front cap apply only to datagrams above `prio_bytes` (256 B): a strict-priority scheduler with size as class. ~8 lines.
- `run.py`: `--state-hz`, `--split-tel`; metrics add `felt_ms`. ~10 lines.

## Cost / complexity

Engineering 4–6 h. Runtime ≈1 h realtime (3 experiments × 2 arms × 20 episodes × ~30 s). Satellite side: a second UDP socket with DSCP EF on the supervisor MCU, no compute, no models; respects SYNTHESIS §5 choice (c).

## Risks and failure modes

- **Priority ends at our egress.** If the choke queue is inside Starlink's mesh, DSCP is not honoured and the saturation benefit shrinks to the head-of-line term (4–26 ms). The nominal −16 ms survives.
- **Synthetic operator overstates the human benefit.** The testbed operator consumes state; a human consumes video and gains earlier state only through the UI (true-pose overlay, link-health, grasp flags). Overlays show no benefit below 150 ms (PMC12788196), so the nominal gain is a human upper bound.
- **Saturation is off-nominal.** SYNTHESIS §1 finds bandwidth non-binding at 4 operators; the regime needs 8 operators, rain, BAROC windows, or a small third-party Starlink allocation.

## Confidence

0.6 overall. 0.8 that felt latency drops ≥15 ms (arithmetic on dwell and serialisation, no behavioural assumption). 0.7 that saturated success is ≥1.3× baseline (the 500 ms point of the curve is well measured; the emulator needs the queue cap first). 0.3 that nominal success or demos/hour improve ≥10 %; the curve is flat below 100 ms and I predict they do not.

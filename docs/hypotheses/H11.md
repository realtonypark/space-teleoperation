# H11: Onboard terminal-grasp primitive (operator streams, satellite finishes the capture)
**Axis:** latency-hiding control. **Author angle:** supervisory/subgoal teleop, onboard-compute branch. Pure goal-click supervision is rejected because it makes the demonstrations robot-generated; only the sub-second segment latency destroys is delegated.

## Claim
On `leo_relay`, letting the satellite close the final ≤8 cm of a capture with a local zero-latency servo, triggered by the operator's own jaw-close command and bounded to ≤2 s, raises capture success by ≥15 points absolute and demos/hour by ≥15 % over baseline, with zero unsafe-motion events and ≤10 % of frames robot-executed.

## Mechanism
The testbed's failure mode is exact: the operator commands "close" when it *sees* the box within 4.5 cm, but the true state has moved on by one loop (RTT + τ_h + playout ≈ 335 ms on `leo_relay`), so the jaw closes outside the 5 cm envelope, the arm bumps the box, and a chase cycle costs seconds. Every SYNTHESIS §3 task has this shape: tolerant approach, then a 100–200 ms-class terminal event that absorbs most of the delay cost (Black 1971, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710009659/downloads/19710009659.pdf). Removing that segment from the delayed loop is the strongest hider in the evidence: Jin et al. 2019, coarse teleop plus local fine controller, 70 → 100 % and 70 → 90 % on a ~111 ms link; copilot plus admittance, nut threading 49 → 65–81 %, downstream imitation learning 7/20 → 18/20 on the assisted data (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17016v2). SUPVIS-Justin at 832 ms RTT is the flown precedent (https://elib.dlr.de/130830/1/RA-L2019_final_Schmaus-Meteron_Supvis_Justin.pdf). The human still produces every approach and carry; the primitive is ~1 s of a 10–20 s episode.

## How the testbed falsifies it
Task `capture`, seeds 0–49 per cell, `--max-s 30`, τ_h 0.25, cmd 50 Hz, tel 30 Hz. Cells: {baseline, H11, H11-ground} × four profiles, plus an RTT sweep 0–1000 ms in 100 ms steps. H11-ground runs the same primitive in `ground_step` from delayed telemetry; H11 − H11-ground is the measured value of onboard compute. One cell adds 5 mm noise and 33 ms lag to the pose the primitive sees (onboard-vision stand-in).

REJECT if any of: (a) on `leo_relay`, success gain < 10 points with the 95 % binomial CI covering zero, or demos/hour gain < 10 %; (b) any SYNTHESIS §6 unsafe-motion event (setpoint change in hold, velocity above clamp at hand-back, keep-out exit, cage contact); (c) robot-executed frames > 25 % of successful episodes; (d) H11-ground within 5 points of H11 on `leo_relay`; (e) SAL/LDLJ of H11 successes worse than baseline's.

## Implementation sketch
- `strategies/terminal_grasp.py` (~50 lines): `TerminalGrasp(Baseline)`. `sat_step` runs the baseline interpolator first. Trigger: newest setpoint commands jaw closed, not grasped, local box-to-grasp-site distance < 0.08 m. Action: one damped-least-squares step per cycle from true `d.qpos` toward the box at ≤7 cm/s with ease-off, jaw closed. Exit: grasped → 0.5 s blend back to the operator stream; 2 s elapsed; distance > 0.08 m; or hold (empty buffer → freeze). `GroundTerminalGrasp` (~20 lines) for the ablation.
- `sim/__init__.py`: move `_dls` here from `ground/operators.py` for reuse.
- `sat/controller.py`: one line after `sim.reset`: `strategy.sat = (m, d, st)`. **The hook does not fit as-is**: `sat_step(buf, now)` carries no local state and the primitive needs the satellite's own joint and object pose. This line is the whole deviation.
- `proto`: `F_ASSIST = 1 << 4`, ORed into telemetry flags and recorded per row (3 lines) so training can mask the segment.
- `ground/operators.py`: on entering `carry`, resync `self.qc` to the seen `tel["q"]` (1 line), as a human watching video would.

## Cost / complexity
Engineering ≈ 8–12 h including the ~600-episode realtime run (~3 h wall); runtime one 3×6 Jacobian solve per cycle. **This is the onboard-compute branch**: SYNTHESIS §5 excludes any subgoal primitive and vision from the baseline. Flight needs a ≥30 Hz onboard object-pose estimate on the Jetson (fiducial or known-object) and a Jetson → supervisor setpoint path; the supervisor keeps its timeout, envelopes and clamps on the primitive's output, so a Jetson SEFI degrades to hold. Ground-truth pose makes the sim result an upper bound; the noise cell bounds the gap.

## Risks and failure modes
1. **Data value.** The grasp segment is robot-executed; policies may learn the primitive, not the human. Mitigation: `assist` flag plus the copilot evidence; still SYNTHESIS §9 open question 4.
2. **Baseline already passes.** The 5 cm kinematic envelope is generous; if baseline reaches ~90 % of zero on `leo_relay`, the gain appears only at ≥300 ms and criterion (a) fails.
3. **Hand-back jump.** The leader pose lags the arm after the primitive moves it; without blend and resync this trips the velocity-clamp criterion.
4. **Physics proxy.** MuJoCo has no restitution, so the bump-and-chase penalty is understated; real gain is likely larger.
5. **Trigger tuning.** 8 cm and 2 s are `[design choice]`; too wide and the primitive grabs unintended boxes.

## Confidence
0.55 that it beats baseline by the claimed margin on `leo_relay`; 0.8 on `geo_relay`. The mechanism has the strongest evidence of any hider in the reports and removes exactly the testbed's failure mode, but baseline `leo_relay` success on this task is unmeasured and may already sit near the acceptance line.

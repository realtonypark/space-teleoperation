# H12: Satellite-side dead reckoning of the command stream (negative playout delay)
**Axis:** latency-hiding control. **Author angle:** satellite-side dead-reckoning/extrapolation of the command stream plus jitter-buffer smoothing.

## Claim
Replacing the baseline's lagged linear interpolation with a least-squares linear extrapolation of the sequence-ordered setpoint stream, evaluated one uplink-plus-playout interval ahead, removes 45–65 ms from the operator's loop on `leo_relay` and raises demos/hour by ≥10 % at unchanged safety. It buys nothing on `geo_relay`.

## Mechanism
The baseline renders the buffer as it stood `interp_s` ago, so the arm trails the operator's hand by uplink delay + playout lag (30 + 30 ms in SYNTHESIS §8; 30 + 20 ms in the current code). That segment is pure onset latency, and Rakita et al. (HRI 2020, https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2020/RMG20/rmg-hri2020.pdf) showed operators adapt to slowness but never to onset latency. The command stream is predictable: a leader arm or the synthetic operator moves at ≤0.07 m/s with ease-off over the last 3.5 cm, so accelerations are ~0.06 m/s² and the second-order extrapolation error over 60 ms is ~0.1 mm. Dead reckoning of this kind is standard in distributed simulation (IEEE 1278.1 DIS, https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/1278.1/4949/), and FoReCo cut trajectory error ×18 in simulation and ×2 on hardware by inferring missing commands instead of freezing (arXiv 2205.04189, teleop_fundamentals §2).

The extrapolator is also the jitter buffer. Fitting a line over the last K = 8 packets in sequence-number time (`seq / cmd_hz`, no clock sync needed) smooths jitter and single losses, and sequence ordering removes the baseline's backward tick on reordered packets (it sorts by arrival time, so a late old packet becomes "newest"). Beech et al. 2024 (DOI 10.1145/3701984) found mean latency dominates jitter, so trading positive buffer depth for negative depth is the right direction. MacKenzie & Ware 1993 (https://www.yorku.ca/mack/CHI93b.html) give the payoff slope: +64 % movement time at 225 ms lag, ≈0.28 %/ms.

Smoothing is not optional: two-point extrapolation of the operator's 1.5 mrad per-tick noise over 60 ms injects 6.4 mrad (4× raw); an 8-point least-squares slope injects 0.7 mrad.

## How the testbed falsifies it
Profiles `leo_relay`, `direct_gs`, `geo_relay`; task `capture`; seeds 0–29; `tau_h` 0.25; `cmd_hz` 50. Arms: Baseline vs H12 with L ∈ {0, 30, 60, 90} ms and K ∈ {4, 8}. L = 0, K = 8 isolates smoothing/ordering from extrapolation. Effective latency is measured offline as the cross-correlation lag between `action` and `observation.state` in the recorded episode (both already logged); prediction: H12 lag = baseline lag − L ± 10 ms.

REJECT if, at the best L on `leo_relay`, demos/hour is not ≥10 % above baseline with the 95 % bootstrap CI excluding zero, or success rate falls by >3 pts, or any unsafe-motion event occurs (setpoint changes while `F_SAFETY_HOLD` is set; joint velocity above clamp at resume), or `jerk_sum_sq` exceeds 2× baseline. Also reject if the measured lag reduction is <0.5 L: the extrapolation is then being clamped, not applied.

## Implementation sketch
`spaceteleop/strategies/deadreckon.py`, ~40 lines, subclass of `Strategy`; only `sat_step` overridden, `ground_step`, runner, controller and proto unchanged.

```
buf entries (t_arr, seq, sp) -> keep last K by seq
time base: t_i = seq_i * dt              (dt = 1/cmd_hz, class attr; plumb --cmd-hz: 1 line)
eval time: t* = seq_n*dt + (now - t_arr_n) + L
per joint: LS line over K points, slope clamped to v_max, evaluated at t*
horizon:   now - t_arr_n > H (100 ms) -> freeze at last value (baseline gap behaviour)
```
The gripper joint passes through unextrapolated (a step command). H < `timeout_s`, so hold semantics are the baseline's.

## Cost / complexity
Engineering: 4–6 h including the lag metric and sweep script. Runtime: 7 slope fits per control tick. Satellite side: a deterministic playout filter on the supervisor MCU, an amendment to SYNTHESIS §5 item 1 with no perception, model or environment state. It does alter the commanded trajectory by up to v_max × L, so if the §5 exclusion is read strictly this is the smallest possible "onboard compute" branch and should be labelled as such.

## Risks and failure modes
- **Reversal overshoot.** A sharp reversal is extrapolated for L before correction; bounded to v_max × L ≈ 4 mm, but a real leader arm accelerates harder than the synthetic operator. Sweep L downward if jerk rises.
- **Loss bursts.** Bad-state runs of 5+ packets exceed H and fall back to freeze; the gain reverts to baseline, not below it.
- **Testbed optimism.** The human benefit is inferred via MacKenzie & Ware, not measured.
- **Stacking.** Orthogonal to H09, H11 and H14; it conflicts only with H08, which adds buffer depth where this removes it.

## Confidence
0.5 that it beats baseline demos/hour by ≥10 % on `leo_relay`. The loop reduction (≈18 %) is arithmetic and the operator model's overshoot scales with it; the doubt is whether an 18 % cut lands on the steep part of the success-vs-latency curve for this task, which is what the sweep measures.

# H10: Ground digital twin as the operator's view

**Axis:** latency-hiding control. **Author angle:** model-mediated teleop: ground twin of object + arm; satellite tracks twin-verified setpoints.

## Claim

If the operator acts on a ground twin (arm = setpoints already in flight, object = ballistic extrapolation of the last telemetry) instead of on delayed telemetry, then at RTT ≥ 500 ms demos/hour rises ≥ 15 % versus baseline with no loss of success rate or safety. It does not claim > 5 % on `leo_relay`.

## Mechanism

The baseline operator keeps commanding toward the goal until it *sees* arrival, so it overshoots by speed × perceived lag (`ground/operators.py`), bumps the free-floating box and chases it. Perceived lag = RTT + dwell + playout + human reaction ≈ 0.35 s on `leo_relay`, ≈ 0.85 s on `geo_relay`: 2.4 cm and 6 cm of overshoot at 0.07 m/s against a 4.5 cm close-at distance.

A twin removes the arm half of that lag exactly, because the arm's near future is fixed by commands already in the pipeline. This is the Bejczy/Kim phantom robot (±5 mm placement at multi-second delay, https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/258061/) and Mitra & Niemeyer's model-mediated teleoperation (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0278364904045563). The object half is where µg is kind to a model: a free body without contact moves at constant velocity, so extrapolation error ≈ 1 cm/s × 0.3 s = 3 mm. Measured returns for this hider class: MMT at 500 ms one-way, completion time not different from zero delay (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.07922v1); predictive display at 500 ms, speed +29 %, path deviation −35 % (secondary, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343874282); −19 % time at 1 s (https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08627); no benefit at 150 ms (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12788196/). The last point scopes the claim away from `leo_relay`.

"Twin-verified" is ground-side only: each setpoint is clipped to the twin's joint clamps and keep-out box before sending, the Astrobee/Canadarm2 pre-validated-motion pattern (space_teleop_prior_art §Implication). The satellite stays at SYNTHESIS §5 option (c).

## Expected effect (quantified)

Baseline measured today (3 seeds, capture v0, `tau_h` 0.25 s, placeholder profiles):

| Profile | RTT p50 | Success | Mean duration | Demos/h (% of zero) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `zero` | 10 ms | 3/3 | 6.00 s | 600 (100 %) |
| `leo_relay` | 89 ms | 3/3 | 6.34 s | 568 (95 %) |
| `geo_relay` | 512 ms | 3/3 | 7.75 s | 464 (77 %) |

Predicted with the twin, same seeds:

- `geo_relay`: duration 7.75 → 6.3–6.8 s, demos/h 530–570 (88–95 % of zero), +15–25 %; success unchanged at the 5 cm envelope. With the planned 20 mm jaw, baseline success predicted < 50 % (6 cm overshoot) and twin ≥ 80 % `[extrapolated]`.
- Sweep 750–1000 ms: baseline 55–65 % of zero demos/h (linear-in-delay, Sheridan & Ferrell 1963, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19660064449); twin 80–90 %. ±10 points.
- `leo_relay`: +0–5 %, inside noise. Not claimed.
- Effective latency: perceived arm lag (≈ 105 ms leo, ≈ 550 ms geo) → ≈ 0; object lag → 3 mm prediction error.
- Safety events zero in both arms; bandwidth unchanged.

## How the testbed falsifies it

Profiles `zero`, `leo_relay`, `geo_relay` plus a symmetric sweep at 0/100/200/300/500/750/1000 ms; task capture (and peg-in-hole when it exists); `tau_h` 0.25; seeds 0–19; `Baseline` vs `Twin` on identical seeds. REJECT if any of: (a) at `geo_relay` or any sweep point ≥ 500 ms, twin demos/h < 1.10 × baseline; (b) twin success below baseline anywhere; (c) any unsafe-motion event or hold-count increase; (d) twin jerk or SAL worse by > 20 % (smoothness is a curation signal, data_pipeline §2). A null on `leo_relay` does not reject.

## Implementation sketch

`spaceteleop/strategies/twin.py`, ≈ 80–120 lines, subclassing `Strategy`:

- `ground_step(tel, sp)`: log `(now, sp)`; clip `sp` to joint ranges and the keep-out box using the ground `MjModel`; return it. `sat_step` = `Baseline.sat_step`.
- `observe(tel, now)`: replace `tel["q"]` with the setpoint logged at `now − tau_h` (the phantom the human saw); replace `tel["obj"]` with the last two frames' finite-difference velocity extrapolated by `tau_h + OWD_down + dwell + OWD_up + interp_s` (OWD from the echoed `last_cmd_t_send`); freeze and flag when telemetry age > `timeout_s`, mirroring the satellite hold so the operator stops commanding into a blackout.

It does **not** fit the hook exactly: `ground_step` runs after `operator.step(seen, dt)` has consumed the delayed frame, and MMT acts upstream of the operator. It needs one line in `ground/loop.py` (`seen = strategy.observe(seen, now)`) plus an identity default in `base.py`. No runner, proto, link or satellite change.

## Cost / complexity

≈ 8 h: 3 h strategy + self-check, 1 h seam, 4 h sweeps (realtime: 2 arms × 10 conditions × 20 seeds × ≈ 8 s ≈ 1 h wall). Runtime: one extra `mj_forward` per ground tick. Satellite: nothing; respects SYNTHESIS §5 (c), not the onboard-compute branch.

## Risks and failure modes

- **Contact breaks the object model.** After a bump the twin is wrong until the next frame (≤ 33 ms + OWD_down). Re-anchor every frame; snap when innovation > 2 cm. The twin helps free space only.
- **Servo mismatch on hardware.** The testbed twin shares the MuJoCo actuator, so it overstates fidelity; a Dynamixel lags 3–8 ms plus settling. Keep a first-order servo-lag knob.
- **Leader-follower rigs already put a phantom in the hand.** The increment is object prediction and jaw timing; smaller if the grasp event is a small part of the episode.
- **Synthetic-operator flattery.** A scripted operator benefits mechanically from a correct display; the geo gain with humans may be smaller.

## Confidence

0.6 that it beats baseline by ≥ 15 % demos/h at ≥ 500 ms RTT: the mechanism is exact for the arm, near-exact for a ballistic object, the measured baseline loss at 512 ms is 23 points, and the literature recovers most of that at 500 ms. 0.15 that it moves anything on `leo_relay`, hence not claimed.

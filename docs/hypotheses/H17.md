NO HYPOTHESIS: Task selection by segmentability cannot beat the baseline on the acceptance profile, and I measured this rather than argued it. The program's operating point is a ≈333 ms loop on `leo_relay` (SYNTHESIS §8), and the literature places the payoff of explicit segmentation (move-and-wait, Sheridan & Ferrell 1963, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19660064449; Black 1971, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710009659/downloads/19710009659.pdf) and of every other pause-then-commit scheme at ≥0.5–1 s, with Rakita et al. 2020 (https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2020/RMG20/rmg-hri2020.pdf) finding no operator strategy that recovers onset latency at 250–750 ms. To check whether a settle-gate plus ballistic-grasp protocol could still pay on our task, I ran an in-process pure-delay sweep (10 seeds, loop delay D = 0–1.5 s, 50 Hz operator, 500 Hz sim; script `sweep.py` in the session scratchpad) of the current continuous-tracking operator against a segmented operator (approach → hold at a standoff until the seen arm has stopped → open-loop move to a drift-extrapolated grasp pose with jaw close → wait one self-measured loop delay for F_GRASPED, retry on miss → carry). Two findings. (1) Task v0 as built cannot falsify any task-design hypothesis: with the 50 mm kinematic capture envelope the baseline is 10/10 at every D up to 1.5 s with zero bumps, and over real sockets it is 4/4 on `zero`, `leo_relay` (89 ms RTT) and `geo_relay` (512 ms RTT) with mean episode time only ×1.28 at 512 ms; the segmented protocol is strictly worse there (+0.9–1.3 s per episode, −17 to −25 % demos/hour, and 8/10 at D ≥ 1 s). (2) With a jaw-scale 20 mm envelope (`GRASP_TOL` 0.02, `close_at` 0.018) the task does become latency-sensitive, but the crossover sits at ≈0.75–1 s: baseline 8/10 (its own zero-delay ceiling, two seeds are geometrically blocked) through D = 0.5 s, then 7/10 at 0.75 s, 3/10 at 1.0 s, 1/10 at 1.5 s; segmented 8/10 at 0–0.15 s, 7/10 at 0.25–0.5 s, 6/10 at 0.75 s, 5/10 at 1.0 s, 4/10 at 1.5 s, at +0.2–1.0 s per successful episode below 0.75 s (−3 to −16 % demos/hour). So segmentation trades throughput for nothing across the whole `leo_relay` envelope, including the +74 ms 15 s spikes, and only wins on `geo_relay`, which SYNTHESIS §10 excludes from continuous control; an adaptive gate keyed on measured RTT would be inert on the acceptance profile and so cannot reach the >10 % bar. The remaining direction on this angle, prefer slow media tasks (1, 3) over free-floating capture, is already SYNTHESIS §3/§10 ground truth and is not falsifiable with the single capture task the testbed has. Note for verifiers: any success-rate hypothesis evaluated on task v0 needs the capture envelope tightened to jaw scale first (one constant in `spaceteleop/sim/__init__.py`, with the operator's `close_at` kept below it), otherwise the success-vs-latency curve is flat to 1.5 s and every hypothesis will appear to pass.

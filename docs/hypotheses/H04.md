NO HYPOTHESIS: Sending every command over two independent links with first-arrival-wins cannot plausibly move a primary metric by >10 % on the ground-truth `leo_relay` profile, for four quantified reasons. (1) Latency: both paths share the 30/18 ms base, so first-arrival only trims jitter; the expected minimum of two i.i.d. draws with SD 14 ms up and 11 ms down is ≈0.56σ ≈ 8 + 6 = 14 ms lower, plus perhaps 5–10 ms of playout buffer that could be shaved, i.e. ≈20–25 ms out of the 333 ms operator loop (SYNTHESIS §8), taking the machine loop from 163 to ≈140 ms, still inside the same <200 ms "ideal" band, so the predicted success-rate change is within run-to-run noise (teleop_fundamentals §1.2, https://www.nitindermohan.com/documents/2024/pubs/starlinkWWW2024.pdf for the jitter moments). (2) Loss: the 1.1 % background and the 1 s/30 % boundary bursts are already covered by same-path temporal duplicate-send at ≤0.2 Mbps per operator (multi_operator_bandwidth §2), so spatial diversity adds nothing measurable. (3) The +74 ms uplink spike every 15 s is globally synchronised across the constellation (Mohan et al. §1.1; Casparsen et al. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08439), so two terminals on the same spacecraft see it together and first-arrival cannot trim it. (4) Outages: at 1.7 h⁻¹ with 87 % under 2 s (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13402), 30 s episodes expose ≈1.4 % of episodes and 120 s episodes ≈5.7 %; even if every exposed episode fails and the second link masks 100 % of them, the ceiling on success and demos/hour is 1.4–5.7 %, and that ceiling itself assumes terminal-independent outages, which is `[unverified]` because both terminals traverse the same mesh, gateway and PoP. Exceeding 10 % would need an ISL-relayed outage rate ≥2–7× the consumer figure, which is exactly open question §9.1 with no measurement, so the hypothesis would rest on an invented number. A truly independent second path (Starlink + direct GS) exists only 21 % of the time and saves ≈18 ms RTT in-pass. The dual-terminal question is better answered as a sensitivity sweep of outage rate inside the baseline experiment than as a head-to-head strategy.

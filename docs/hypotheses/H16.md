# H16: Arm pool with ground-scripted resets (N arms > M operators)

**Axis:** operator interface and workflow. **Author angle:** multi-operator arm sharing, time-multiplexed so live video per operator is constant and reset time is hidden.

## Claim

With more arms than operators (N = 6–8 for M = 4), a ground broker that hands each operator a freshly reset arm the moment their episode ends, while the arm they just used is reset by a ground-scripted open-loop trajectory, raises successful demos per operator-hour by >25 % on `leo_relay` and per pass on `direct_gs`, at unchanged success rate and zero added unsafe-motion events. It changes neither effective latency nor outage behaviour.

## Mechanism

Reset is a large share of wall clock: ALOHA got 10–20 min of data per task from 30–60 min "because of resets and teleoperator mistakes" (data_pipeline §2.1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705); LeRobot defaults to `episode_time_s=60, reset_time_s=60` (https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/il_robots). In µg the reset is arm motion (return the object to free float, retract), so it costs link time but needs no human: a pre-validated trajectory streamed from the ground, the Canadarm2/Astrobee pattern (space_teleop_prior_art §1). The scarce resources are operators and live video (2.6 Mbps per operator with FEC, multi_operator_bandwidth §3), not arms; the program already scopes 4–8 arms for 4 operators. Pooling keeps exactly M arms on live video, under the Astrobee/POIC rule of one write authority per arm granted by a ground broker (multi_operator_bandwidth §4, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180003326/downloads/20180003326.pdf).

Per operator, throughput goes from 1/(T_e + R) to 1/(T_e + s): T_e episode time, R reset time, s hand-off cost. Task-switch costs are 0.2–0.5 s for simple stimulus changes (Monsell 2003, https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00028-7); re-orienting to a new arm's scene is plausibly 1–2 s `[unverified]`. Arms needed: N/M ≥ (T_e + R + s)/(T_e + s).

## Expected effect (quantified)

Gain = (T_e + R)/(T_e + s) − 1. Testbed capture episodes are ≈10–15 s `[unverified]`; take T_e = 12 s, s = 1.5 s:

| R | Gain, demos/operator-hour | Arms for M = 4 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 s | +11 % | 5 |
| 6 s | +33 % | 6 |
| 12 s (ALOHA/LeRobot ratio) | +78 % | 8 |

Same gain on every profile: a throughput multiplier orthogonal to latency. The acceptance ratio vs zero-latency gets slightly worse, since reset no longer dilutes the latency penalty; report absolute demos/operator-hour separately. Success rate ±3 points. Safety events: zero added. Downlink constant at M × 2 streams, so 8 arms on VR stereo fit the 50 Mbps `leo_relay` cap (52 vs 104 Mbps). Uncertainty ±20 points, from R and s.

## How the testbed falsifies it

1. Add a measured reset: after success the box respawns and a `ResetOperator` streams the arm home over the same link; reset ends when the arm is within tolerance of home.
2. 10-minute realtime sessions, M = 4 synthetic operators, profiles `zero`, `leo_relay`, `direct_gs` (9 min on / 35 off stand-in), seeds 0–4, Baseline strategy throughout. Control: N = 4, each operator resets their own arm. Treatment: N = 6 and 8, broker hand-off with a fixed 1.5 s operator blackout plus 1 s video-switch gap `[assumption]`.
3. Metrics: successes per operator-hour and per pass, success rate, unsafe-motion events.

REJECT if the N = 8 gain on `leo_relay` is < 10 %, success rate drops > 5 points, or any hand-off produces a joint-velocity jump or keep-out event. Also REJECT as not worth the arms if measured R < 3 s.

## Implementation sketch

Cannot fit the Strategy hook: `Strategy` maps one operator to one arm's setpoint stream; this is scheduling above the runner. The Strategy is untouched, so it composes with any latency hider.

- `sim`: `respawn_box(m, d, st, seed)`, box only, ~10 lines.
- `ground/operators.py`: `ResetOperator` reusing `servo()` toward the home keyframe, ~15 lines.
- `run.py`: `--arms N --operators M --session-s T`; N arm slots (socket pair, Link, sat thread), M operator threads pulling from a ready queue, a reset worker per finished arm, demos/operator-hour metric, ~80–100 lines.
- Flight: the broker also switches the Jetson video mux (SYNTHESIS §4).

## Cost / complexity

Engineering 8–12 h. Runtime ≈7.5 h wall (3 profiles × 3 N × 5 seeds × 10 min); 8 MuJoCo instances in threads may hit the GIL, then sat threads become subprocesses (a launcher change per run.py). Satellite side: respects the §5 onboard decision; resets are ground-streamed setpoints under the same buffer/hold/envelope logic. Hardware: 6–8 arms instead of 4, the existing scale branch (hardware_landscape §3).

## Risks and failure modes

- **R is task-dependent.** Capture reset may be 3–5 s; granular or liquid pour-back is a full task. Gain scales with R.
- **Hand-off cost s is unmeasured.** Synthetic operators switch for free; at 3–5 s real re-orientation the gain halves.
- **Outages are common-mode.** All arms share one link; pooling hides none of the 1.7 h⁻¹ outages, unlike the SYNTHESIS §10 seed of handing off "during holds".
- **Hand-off jump.** The new operator's first command must start from the arm's current pose; a parked arm makes stale telemetry true, but the safety criterion must check it.

## Confidence

0.7 that it beats +25 % on demos/operator-hour at `leo_relay` with N = 8, given R ≥ 6 s; the arithmetic is certain, the number rides on measured R and the assumed 1.5 s hand-off. 0.9 that it clears +10 % if R ≥ 3 s. Near 0 that it moves success rate or effective latency, and I claim neither.

## Implication for our design

Size the flight system at 8 arms for 4 operators with broker and reset scripts on the ground; the satellite baseline is unchanged. Measure R in the testbed first, since the whole gain rides on it.

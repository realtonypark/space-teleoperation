What the second wave improves

The goal is unchanged: four concurrent ground operators and four to eight simple arms inside a rideshare-class LEO spacecraft. The review now separates a task completed in a simulator from a usable demonstration, an operable mission and a better learned policy.

Relay performance depends on the task. Capture retains 81.0% of zero-delay success (17/30 versus 21/30), but only 72.5% of gross throughput (153.9 versus 212.4 demos/h), below the 80% point target. The conservative 95% lower bound on its success ratio is 43.9%. Peg retains 30/30 success and 98.3% of gross throughput. Neither task completes the full mission acceptance gate: direct-pass/GEO profiles are absent from this targeted run, and physical safety remains unproven.

Twin helps the delayed Capture proxy, with limited statistical certainty. At 400 ms, success rises from 13/30 to 21/30 and gross rate from 96.5 to 202.8 demos/h. The gross-rate difference is +106.3 [38.1, 187.5] demos/h in a pointwise paired bootstrap interval. Success has Holm-adjusted p = .0645 across six comparisons; it does not cross .05 after adjustment. Twin also improves zero-delay gross rate. After that control, the conditional speed ratio of ratios is 1.105 [0.941, 1.281], which does not establish a ≥10% latency-specific gain.

The clearest corrected effects remain task-specific. Gain reduces 400 ms Capture to 0/30 (Holm p = .000977). Peg at 250 ms improves from 0/30 to 27/30 with DeadReckon (p = 7.45 × 10⁻⁸); at 400 ms, Terminal improves 0/30 to 30/30 (p = 1.12 × 10⁻⁸). TerminalGround remains 0/30. Local and ground assistance still differ in sensing, state age and update rate, so this does not isolate compute placement.

Endpoint review found six of 117 successful Peg records up to 0.088 mm above the specified depth because the primary execution checked the previous pose. The final code checks updated pose and contact; primary outcomes remain tied to their original revision. The frame audit checks target distance and record consistency, not a full replay of every task predicate. The final correction passed 30 fresh endpoints (10 each for baseline zero/relay and Terminal at 400 ms), with final depth, lateral position and fixture contact independently reconstructed from saved state. These are separate regression observations. See the report.

Paired seeds control scenario generation, not identical socket schedules. Capture deadlines are 20 s, except 30 s for the 12 s outage; Peg uses 30 s. Synthetic reaction delay is 170 ms. The relay’s 48 ms nominal RTT is an emulation scenario, not a measured spacecraft service. Bootstrap intervals are pointwise; 30/30 success is not a population guarantee.

Outage exposure is measured. Hold occurs in 29/30 episodes for each forced-outage profile; 29/30 enter the retract-age regime for the 12 s outage. One episode in each cell ends before the outage. The long-outage cell’s sole success is that unexposed episode, so it does not demonstrate successful recovery through the full blackout.

Count demonstrations, including the failures

Active-task rate = 3,600 × successes / sum(all attempt durations)

In the historical Tier-2 400 ms cells, Capture baseline achieved 350.9 successful-attempt demos/h but only 100.8 successes per attempted hour. Peg baseline achieved 302.5 successful-attempt demos/h but just 1.2 successes per attempted hour. The first metric ignores time spent failing.

Even the corrected active-task rate excludes campaign setup, file handling, unmodelled resets, operator time and contact availability. Measure QA-accepted demonstrations per active hour, booked hour and calendar day separately. A high relative score against a weak zero-delay baseline also needs an absolute success requirement.

A mission architecture with open gates

Retain the simple command path: one writer per arm, timestamped setpoints, bounded local playout, an independent deterministic supervisor and video compute outside the safety path. Use direct RF passes as the reference architecture. Keep Starlink and Kepler as conditional relay options.

  • Communications. Obtain client-orbit coverage, terminal resources, directional application delay, burst loss, acquisition behavior and an allocated service. Published optical line rate and commercial announcements do not establish this mission’s usable video bandwidth or continuity.
  • Availability. The four-site study gives 21.4% geometric visibility at a 5° elevation mask and 14.9% at 10° for its specified orbit and week. These are contact opportunities, not booked operator duty. Geometry appendix.
  • Spacecraft fit. Apex lists a 125 kg dry base bus and a 100 kg rideshare payload allowance. The former 100 kg bus assumption and associated price sketch do not close a selected mission configuration. Apex specification.
  • Enclosure and resources. A 300 L cylinder with length twice its diameter is about 576 × 1,152 mm internally, exceeding the cited 550 mm axis-aligned envelope before walls and mounts. Close packing, stored-energy qualification, radiation, peak/orbit-average power, thermal rejection and arm/base momentum budgets.
  • Microgravity cooling. Pressure does not restore terrestrial buoyancy cooling. Provide conductive paths or controlled circulation; isolate and record airflow that can disturb the science task.

Across all 450 recordings, the maximum sampled setpoint rate is 2.0002 rad/s, with no cycles above the 2.1 rad/s tolerance. Actual simulated joint speed reaches 6.584 rad/s (Elbow, Capture 12 s outage, seed 1024, simulation time 19.914 s). These are sampled maxima; unrecorded physics steps can peak higher. Command-limit compliance is therefore not a physical velocity bound. A held setpoint can still permit servo motion; cage contact is not proven harmless; a ten-second retract timeout does not validate the path with an object held. Recovery must work locally when all links fail. Full recording and motion verification.

Make the scientific value testable

The opportunity is sustained, repeatable microgravity data. Existing orbital measurements, drop towers and suborbital flights are valid comparators. The current fixed-base SO-100 model, kinematic Capture grasp and constrained Peg task cannot establish granular, fluid, deformable or floating-base fidelity.

MuJoCo supports elastic contact; the real question is whether calibrated models predict the selected physical task. The proxy’s flight-performance bias can have either sign. MuJoCo contact documentation.

  1. Choose one repeatable cell. Find a contained task with measurable model error, reliable reset and a target policy. Calibrate against available physical data and test held-out conditions.
  2. Validate the data product. Keep observed and applied actions separate. Record relevant contact/force, base motion and environmental state. Define QA acceptance independently of task success.
  3. Run equal-budget learning comparisons. Compare simulation alone, simulation plus existing physical data, and the same total training budget with additional microgravity demonstrations. Hold policy, optimizer and evaluation budget fixed.
  4. Evaluate physical generalization. Split by object/material, geometry and session; keep correlated chains together. Predeclare a useful improvement and acceptable cost per accepted demonstration.

These physical and learning experiments are proposed, not completed. The recorder produces research NPZ with improved provenance. It is not a LeRobot-compatible dataset: a pinned-format export and an actual loader/episode-boundary check are still required. No policy-learning gain is claimed.

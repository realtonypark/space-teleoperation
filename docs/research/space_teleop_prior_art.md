# Space teleoperation prior art

Scope: every flown program in which a human commanded a robot arm across a space link, plus the two questions at the end (arms inside free-flyers; leader-follower / VR data collection in orbit). Figures carry a source or `[unverified]`. Direction matters: most ISS "telerobotics" experiments put the **operator in orbit and the robot on Earth**. Only the rows marked **G→O** are ground operator → orbital robot, which is our case.

## 2. Arms on free-flying satellites teleoperated from ground

Three flown cases: ETS-VII (1997–99, external 2 m arm, 5–7 s via TDRS, direct and bilateral teleop, plus ESA's interactive autonomy) [S6][S7][S8]; Orbital Express (2007, external arm, scripted autonomy with ground gates, no continuous teleop) [S22][S23]; Xiyuan-0 (2026, small-sat flexible arm, hand-controller teleop with sub-second loop over direct passes) [S32][S33]. **No program has flown a manipulator inside a free-flying satellite's own volume for ground teleoperation** — no finding. Every interior-arm case (ROKVISS was exterior; R2, GITAI S1, Tiangong in-cabin arm) lives on a crewed station with relay links.

Latency structure across all rows collapses to two regimes: **direct pass links at 20–30 ms with 4–20 min windows** (ROKVISS, Kontur-2, Xiyuan-0) versus **GEO relay at 0.8–1.1 s continuous with LOS gaps** (every ESA/NASA ISS experiment) and 5–7 s when 1990s ground networking is added (ETS-VII). Nothing in between has been measured in orbit.

## 3. Leader-follower or VR teleoperation for data collection in orbit

**No program has collected robot-learning demonstration data in orbit** — no finding. Nearest precedents:

- Robonaut 2, 2013: astronaut Marshburn wore motion capture and a VR visor **on the ISS** to teleoperate R2 locally, including a leader-follower exercise with a crewmate and grabbing a tumbling tape roll, "a task only demonstrable in microgravity" [S18]. Operator and robot were both in orbit; purpose was proficiency, not datasets.
- Tiangong-2, 2016: VR demonstrations recorded **on Earth** trained an ML policy that ran on the orbital arm [S27]. The data flow is the reverse of ours.
- Xiyuan-0, 2026: hand-controller teleop of an orbital arm from ground, but no statement that trajectories were logged for learning [S33].

## Implication for our design

The flown evidence says the sub-100 ms regime exists only on direct passes (20–30 ms, ~0.1 % loss, 4–20 min windows), and the continuous regime costs 0.8 s plus a tail of 1–3 s spikes and dropouts that broke force coupling in Analog-1 and killed windows in ETS-VII. So our architecture should assume direct-pass windows as the primary demo-collection mode with a leader-follower interface (no force feedback needed, as Interact/Analog-1 show haptics is what becomes unstable), size the onboard baseline exactly like Astrobee/Canadarm2 (hold on LOS, keep-out zones, pre-validated motion), and quantify demo throughput per pass rather than per hour of wall time, because every prior program was window-limited, not latency-limited, once delay was under ~1 s. The gap we fill is real: no one has logged ground-teleoperated demonstrations from an arm inside a free-flyer, and the only free-flyer arm teleop with sub-second loop (Xiyuan-0) published no numbers.
